Bethel Henry Strousberg

A European railway entrepreneur whose industrial system extended across several countries and whose Bohemian holdings included Zbiroh.

Introducing Strousberg

Bethel Henry Strousberg (1823–1884), born Baruch Hirsch Strausberg, came from a Jewish family in Neidenburg, East Prussia. After moving to London he anglicised his name and entered the Church of England. He later became one of the major railway entrepreneurs of the nineteenth century.

A railway network on a European scale

Strousberg organised railway construction across several European states and tied railway building to engineering works, iron and steel production, locomotives and railway equipment. For the Zbiroh research, this matters because his railways, factories, mines, estates and finance formed parts of one larger economic system.

Prussia and Germany

Major railway enterprises included East Prussian and Berlin–Görlitz projects among others.

Beyond Germany

His railway interests extended into Hungary, Romania and Russia.

Industrial integration

Engineering works and heavy industry supported railway construction.

Historical significance

His rise and collapse became part of the nineteenth-century debate over private railway finance and public control.

Strousberg and Zbiroh Castle

The official history of Zbiroh Castle describes Strousberg as the nineteenth-century “Railway King” who transformed the castle into a modern Neo-Renaissance residence. This archive treats the castle as part of the Strousberg-era landscape while keeping the exact architects, contractors, individual interventions, financing and links to surrounding industrial holdings open for documentary reconstruction.
