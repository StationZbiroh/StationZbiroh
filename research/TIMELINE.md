# Historical Timeline — Working Research Framework

This is a research chronology, not a finished narrative. Dates and relationships must be supported by cited evidence before being promoted to **Documented** status.

## I. Strousberg era — nineteenth century

**Research focus:** establish exactly what industrial installations existed at Borek during the Strousberg period; ownership of land and buildings; production; workforce; transport requirements; and relationship to the developing railway.

**Status:** research phase. Detailed chronology requires primary documents.

## II. Post-Strousberg transfers and fragmentation — especially the 1870s

**Research focus:** reconstruct the ownership chain after the Strousberg period, including sales or division of industrial assets and parcels. Particular importance should be given to historical ownership documents showing successive owners, amendments or crossed-out names.

**Status:** plausible framework; exact owners, parcels and dates must be transcribed from primary records.

## III. Brandeis, Eisenschimmel and the Borek industrial enterprise

**Research focus:** determine when and how Brandeis and subsequent partners acquired or developed the Borek operation; reconstruct the legal identities of the businesses; distinguish continuity of companies from continuity of land, buildings, machinery and production.

Known research target: **Železárna Borek a továrna hospodářských strojů, akc. spol. Brandeis–Eisenschimmel**.

**Status:** active archival research.

## IV. Railway growth, Hotel Borek and the substantial station complex — late nineteenth century to 1918

**Central question:** To what extent did Borek industry influence the location, operation, enlargement and facilities of Zbiroh/Zbirov station?

Research should examine freight volumes, company correspondence, railway administrative records, land ownership, accommodation, passenger facilities, railway offices and the relationship between Hotel Borek and railway functions.

The construction and opening chronology of the substantial early-twentieth-century station building must be established precisely from railway/architectural records rather than simplified to a single remembered date.

**Working hypothesis:** before 1918, the industrial complex and railway formed a closely interconnected industrial-transport system. Whether the private enterprise funded, administered or formally participated in the station remains **unverified**.

## V. First World War and dissolution of Austria-Hungary — 1914–1918

**Research focus:** wartime production, railway traffic, military requirements, company administration, ownership and the consequences of the transition from the Austro-Hungarian system to Czechoslovakia.

**Status:** open research question.

## VI. First Czechoslovak Republic — 1918–1929

**Research focus:** company ownership, products, employment, commercial activity, railway freight and changes in the station's role under Czechoslovak administration.

## VII. Depression and industrial recalibration — 1929–late 1930s

The Depression should not automatically be narrated as simple industrial collapse.

**Research questions:** Did the Borek enterprise restructure? Did production change? Did ownership or legal identity change? Were assets sold or transferred? What happened to employment, patents, catalogues and commercial networks?

**Status:** unresolved; documentary reconstruction required.

## VIII. Occupation and Second World War — 1939–1945

This period should integrate several evidence streams without assuming relationships between them until documented:

- Borek factory ownership and wartime production;
- fate of Jewish owners, managers, employees and families;
- railway and freight use during occupation;
- military use of the surrounding landscape;
- Zbiroh castle and reported SS occupation/use;
- wartime airfield/aviation evidence in the wider Borek/Zbiroh area;
- maps, aerial photographs and contemporary administrative records.

Every specific claim about SS activity, railway use, factory production or military movements requires an attributable source.

## IX. Liberation, postwar reorganisation and nationalisation — 1945 onward

**Research focus:** identify nationalisation measures affecting local industry and establish the development of **Zbirovia** in relation to earlier Borek industry.

Key question: did machinery, personnel, products, patents, designs, commercial rights or other assets move between Borek and Zbirovia?

**Status:** hypothesis requiring company, patent and nationalisation records. Similarity or geographical proximity alone is not evidence of transfer.

## X. Dismantling or demolition of the Borek works — approximately early 1950s

A date around **1952** has emerged in the research discussion for dismantling/demolition of the factory complex.

**Status: Unverified.**

The public archive must not present 1952 as a confirmed demolition date until supported by contemporary records, dated photographs, cadastral changes, aerial imagery, demolition records or equivalent evidence.

## XI. Industrial landscape after the factory

Research should document what survived, what disappeared, subsequent land uses, cadastral changes and the physical traces visible in maps, photographs, aerial imagery and the present landscape.

## Parallel chronology: Stanice Zbiroh

The station should have its own parallel chronology so that railway events can be compared with industrial events rather than assuming causation.

Research themes include:

- nineteenth-century railway facilities and freight;
- development of Zbirov/Zbiroh station;
- planning and construction of the substantial early-twentieth-century station complex;
- railway offices, passenger facilities, restaurant and residential accommodation;
- land ownership around the station;
- 1914–1918 transition;
- interwar railway activity;
- occupation and wartime use;
- postwar railway changes;
- later decline/change of railway functions;
- preservation and reuse of the station complex.

---

## Editorial rule

A chronological coincidence is not automatically a causal relationship. Connections between factory, railway, state, military authorities and later enterprises should be stated as fact only where the evidence demonstrates them.
