# Borek Industrial Complex — Evidence-led historical synthesis

*Working research document for the Stanice Zbiroh Historical Research Archive.*

## Scope and method

This document brings together currently identified sources concerning the Borek/Kařez industrial complex, its relationship to Bethel Henry Strousberg, the later Brandeis and Eisenschimmel enterprises, Hotel Borek, and Zbiroh railway station. It distinguishes contemporary or institutional evidence from later historical summaries. Where sources conflict, the conflict is retained rather than silently resolved.

## 1. Strousberg and the creation of the industrial landscape

A peer-reviewed article in *Zprávy památkové péče* by David Tuma states that Bethel Henry Strousberg purchased the Zbiroh, Osek and Mirošov estates in 1868 and sought to transform the Zbiroh area into an industrial centre. The article specifically identifies the planned Strousberg railway as a link between industrial areas at Františkov near Zbiroh and Borek near Kařez.

Source: https://zpp.npu.cz/en/artkey/zpp-201305-0003_industrial-complexes-technical-monuments-and-structures-related-to-the-strousberg-railway-in-the-podbrdy-area.php?l=en

A detailed later historical account of the Borek works states that the factory was partially brought into operation in 1869. It describes a large steelworks with puddling furnaces, rolling facilities, steam power and a planned Bessemer steelworks. According to that account, the 1875 public-auction record described seven halls containing extensive rolling and puddling equipment, steam hammers, mechanical workshops and an unfinished Bessemer plant.

Source: https://podzemi.solvayovylomy.cz/zcest/str1/str1.htm

The same source describes Strousberg's proposed settlement at Kařez — sometimes called Strousbergstadt — as a planned industrial town intended for approximately 5,000 employees, with worker and official housing, offices, a hospital, baths and a school. Only a fraction of the proposed development was completed.

## 2. Collapse, auction and post-Strousberg ownership

The later historical account states that the Borek factory was purchased at auction in 1875 by Mančík and Šlesinger; that Roubíček and Brandeis acquired it in 1881; and that E. Brandeis acquired Roubíček's share in 1887. It further states that the former rolling mill was converted into an iron foundry and production focused on machine tools.

Source: https://podzemi.solvayovylomy.cz/zcest/str1/str1.htm

These ownership stages should ultimately be checked against the original auction, land-register and company-register records. They are retained here as a sourced historical reconstruction, not as a substitute for those primary records.

## 3. Hotel Borek and the railway landscape

A regional historical account drawing on the Kařez municipal chronicle states that Hotel Borek was built around 1871 by Strousberg during development of the industrial settlement. It records a restaurant on the ground floor and guest rooms above, and says the building later came into the ownership of E. Brandeis. The same account describes Hotel Borek as part of the landscape immediately surrounding Zbiroh station.

Source: https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/7b37e9b1520149ffbfbff4f912411e21

A recent photographic historical feature also identifies Hotel Borek as having served as a provisional station building and reproduces historical material showing the intended connection of Strousberg's railway with the Czech Western Railway at Zbiroh. This is useful corroborative visual evidence, though the underlying archival items should be cited directly wherever possible.

Source: https://www.idnes.cz/cestovani/po-cesku/zanikle-trate-96-stroussbergova-zeleznicni-trat-brdy-zbiroh-zajecov-strasice.A251002_205339_po-cesku_vrja/foto

## 4. Brandeis, Eisenschimmel and conversion into a joint-stock company

A contemporary German technical publication, *Elektrotechnische Rundschau*, reports the corporate reorganisation directly. It states that the Borek ironworks near Zbiroh belonging to E. Brandeis and the agricultural-machinery factory of Franz Eisenschimmel & Co. in Roudnice (Raudnitz) were converted, with participation of the Prague branch of the Anglo-Bank, into a joint-stock company for foundry and agricultural machinery, with its seat at Borek.

Crucially, this contemporary report states that the capital was **2.4 million crowns**.

Source PDF: https://repozytorium.biblos.pk.edu.pl/redo/resources/47976/file/scans/OCR_rezultaty/300000015564_A_v1_200dpi_q60.pdf

This source is especially important because it independently confirms the combination of the two industrial businesses and provides a contemporary capital figure.

### Capitalisation discrepancy

A working AI analysis of the surviving Borek factory catalogue, attributed to Gemini, gave a figure of **2.6 million Czechoslovak crowns**. At present that figure does not have an identified independent citation in this archive. The contemporary German technical publication gives **2.4 million crowns**. The archive therefore records both claims but gives greater evidential weight to the contemporary 2.4-million-crown report until further primary evidence is found.

No modern-dollar corporate valuation should be presented as a historical fact. Any such figure is necessarily an interpretive comparison rather than an archival valuation.

## 5. Evidence of market status

An official 1919 Czech publication lists an enterprise under the name **“Akciová slévárna železa a továrna na stroje ku vyrábění nástrojů Brandeis, Eisenschimmel, Borek v Čechách”** in a table of ironworks and engineering companies, with a quoted figure of 220 in the publication's market/course column.

Source PDF: https://data.atlascloud.cz/LCR/Rejstrik/Sb/public/1919/sb044-1919/217_1919/217_1919.pdf

This is significant evidence that the Brandeis–Eisenschimmel enterprise was being recorded in contemporary financial/commercial material. The exact meaning and market context of the quoted 220 should be established from the publication itself before drawing conclusions about exchange listing, share price or valuation.

## 6. Agricultural machinery and commercial reach

A digitised Ljubljana trade-fair publication lists **“Železarna Borek a továrna hospodářských strojů, akc. spol.”** of Borek, Czechoslovakia, in connection with threshing machinery. This provides independent evidence of the company's agricultural-machinery identity and presence in a wider Central European commercial context.

Source PDF: https://www.dlib.si/stream/URN%3ANBN%3ASI%3ADOC-Y0XDP006/d6ed5635-9cf4-4610-b570-6088f9705622/PDF

## 7. The surviving factory catalogue

The archive holds a digital lead to a technical and commercial catalogue of **Železárna Borek a továrna hospodářských strojů, akc. spol. (Brandeis–Eisenschimmel)**, provisionally dated to the 1920s–1930s.

Catalogue PDF: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1OUb7eF4BrTwoxXD5pRuFJL9g8i5m8SbD/view?usp=drivesdk

The current working analysis of the catalogue identifies a telegraphic ordering key, standardised product codes, installation and repair arrangements, a six-month warranty, foundry and machine-tool production, agricultural machinery, elevators and mixers, and references interpreted as evidence of wider industrial/social infrastructure. These claims remain subject to page-by-page verification against the catalogue itself.

The working catalogue analysis is attributed to **Gemini** and is deliberately kept distinct from independently verified archival conclusions.

## 8. Scale of employment and the Depression

A digitised regional chronicle/publication states that the Borek ironworks employed approximately **420 workers and 45 officials in 1920** and that production stopped in **1932**. It also connects the factory with characteristic workers' houses in Kařez and describes the closure as having consequences for local cultural and community life.

Source: https://fliphtml5.com/fefel/eiqu/Kronika_obce_Bujesily/

The later Strousberg/Borek historical account likewise dates the stoppage of factory operations to the economic crisis in 1932 and states that the site was subsequently used for grain storage.

Source: https://podzemi.solvayovylomy.cz/zcest/str1/str1.htm

Together these sources support treating the Depression as a distinct phase in the history of the industrial complex, while the precise legal sequence of insolvency, liquidation, ownership transfer and later reuse still requires company-register and archival documentation.

## 9. Later use and surviving fabric

The later historical account states that grain storage followed the factory closure and that a concrete silo was constructed in the 1950s. A recent photographic feature identifies the surviving administrative building of the former Borek ironworks and states that it dates back to the Strousberg industrial complex.

Sources:

- https://podzemi.solvayovylomy.cz/zcest/str1/str1.htm
- https://www.idnes.cz/cestovani/po-cesku/zanikle-trate-96-stroussbergova-zeleznicni-trat-brdy-zbiroh-zajecov-strasice.A251002_205339_po-cesku_vrja/foto

The exact chronology of demolition or dismantling of the wider factory complex remains unresolved and should not yet be assigned a definitive date.

## 10. Questions still open

The following remain active research questions:

- the complete ownership chain between Strousberg's collapse and the Brandeis period, verified from primary land/company records;
- the exact legal date and structure of the Brandeis–Eisenschimmel joint-stock reorganisation;
- reconciliation of the 2.4-million and 2.6-million crown capitalisation claims;
- the precise relationship between the factory, Hotel Borek and Zbiroh station at different periods;
- whether and how the industrial enterprise contributed financially or administratively to the 1911–1912 station complex;
- the factory's specific production during the German occupation and Second World War;
- the legal liquidation history of the company during and after the Depression;
- the exact date and circumstances in which most of the factory complex was dismantled or demolished;
- identification and dating of all surviving factory structures;
- full bibliographic description and provenance of the surviving factory catalogue.

## Source hierarchy used by the archive

1. Original archival documents, registers, catalogues, maps and contemporary publications.
2. Institutional and peer-reviewed historical research.
3. Regional histories that identify their underlying sources.
4. Later secondary summaries and journalistic reconstructions.
5. AI-generated analysis, retained as a research aid but not treated as evidence by itself.

The archive's governing principle remains: **the strength of the language must not exceed the strength of the evidence.**
