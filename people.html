Josef Švejkovský

A Kařez tool manufacturer documented immediately after the Second World War, and a possible key figure in the transition from the Brandeis factory era to post-war industrial administration.

Primary evidence: 7 June 1945

The printed letterhead reads: “Josef Svejkovský — Továrna na nástroje — Kařez” and gives Zbiroh railway station and telephone number Zbiroh 6 b. It was addressed to Železářská obchodní společnost, akc. spol., for the attention of Mr Schejbal, in Plzeň.

The date is historically significant. On 7 June 1945, only weeks after the end of the war in Europe, the Kařez factory was corresponding under Josef Svejkovský’s name and presenting Zbiroh station as its railway connection.

The envelope proves commercial identity and activity at that moment. It does not by itself establish who owned the factory during the occupation, when Svejkovský assumed control, or whether it was the legal successor to the Brandeis–Eisenschimmel enterprise.

Likely biographical match

The Czechoslovak Legionary Community records a Josef Svejkovský/Švejkovský, born in Zbiroh on 24 July 1892, domiciled in Zbiroh and Kařez, and employed as a nástrojař (toolmaker). He was captured in 1915 and entered the Czechoslovak Legions in 1917. The agreement of name, locality and occupation makes identification with the factory operator highly plausible, but a second identifying document is still required. View the legionary record.

An archival inventory for the Rokycany Tax Administration independently lists “Josef Svejkovský, výroba nástrojů, Kařez” under reference N 15, inventory no. 234. The underlying file is a priority research target. View the archival inventory.

1948 court-related index entry

A surviving working register of the criminal investigation commission of the District National Committee in Rokycany contains the entry “Svejkovský Josef, Kařez — Tr. C 49/1948” and points to page 58 of the register. View the digitised working register.

What this proves: a case or file bearing Josef Svejkovský’s name and the locality Kařez was indexed under reference Tr. C 49/1948. What it does not prove: the register alone does not reveal the allegation, Svejkovský’s role in the proceeding, whether the matter reached a court hearing, or its outcome. He could have been accused, investigated, a witness, cleared, or recorded in another capacity.

Is this the same Josef Svejkovský?

The agreement of the full name and the small locality of Kařez makes identification with the tool manufacturer documented in 1945 a strong working possibility. It is not yet proven. Confirmation requires the underlying file—especially any stated date of birth, occupation, address, factory connection or signature—to be compared with the 1945 envelope, the legionary record and the Rokycany tax file.

Identity unresolved · underlying file required

Open hypothesis · wartime and post-war transition

What role did Svejkovský play?

As an experienced local toolmaker, Svejkovský would probably have possessed valuable knowledge of local workers, suppliers, machinery and transport. Such knowledge could have made him useful to private owners, occupation authorities, national administrators or the restored Czechoslovak state. Local expertise alone is not evidence of collaboration.

The working hypothesis is that Svejkovský may have operated, administered, acquired or restarted the Kařez tool factory during the wartime or immediate post-war transition. Determining which of these roles he held could explain the succession from the Brandeis factory to later nationalised organisations.

What could support it

  • A wartime company-register entry naming him as owner, manager or administrator.
  • Factory correspondence, payrolls, contracts or occupation-era production records.
  • A transfer deed linking the Brandeis–Eisenschimmel assets to Svejkovský.
  • The underlying 1948 district investigation file, if it concerns factory control or nationalisation.

What could disprove or revise it

  • Evidence that he acquired or restarted the factory only after liberation.
  • Records showing he operated a separate Kařez workshop unrelated to the Brandeis works.
  • Evidence that another administrator controlled the factory throughout the occupation.
  • A 1948 file concerning an unrelated matter or clearing him of an allegation.

Essential caution: an index entry for “Svejkovský Josef, Kařez — Tr. C 49/1948” proves only that a file existed. It does not reveal whether he was accused, a witness, cleared, or mentioned in another capacity. No claim of collaboration or wrongdoing should be made unless the underlying record supports it.

Where was the tool factory?

The envelope establishes the business locality as Kařez and gives Zbiroh railway station as its transport connection, but it does not provide a building number or street address. This distinction matters: “Stanice dráhy Zbiroh” tells customers and carriers which station served the factory; it does not prove that the workshop stood inside the station building.

There were relatively few substantial buildings in the immediate station and Borek industrial area. That makes the unidentified factory site a potentially answerable geographical question. Svejkovský may have used the former Brandeis–Eisenschimmel factory, one surviving workshop within that complex, a building near Hotel Borek, or a separate smaller workshop elsewhere in Kařez. At present the archive cannot choose among these possibilities.

Evidence needed to locate it

  • A company registration giving a house or parcel number.
  • The tax file N 15, inventory no. 234.
  • Wartime and post-war cadastral records and property ledgers.
  • Fire-insurance plans, machinery inventories and nationalisation schedules.
  • Telephone directories identifying subscriber “Zbiroh 6 b.”
  • Freight records showing where consignments were collected or delivered.

Questions for buildings at the station

  • Which buildings existed between 1939 and 1953?
  • Who owned and occupied each building?
  • Which contained workshops, accommodation, offices or storage?
  • How did people and goods move between factory, hotel and railway?
  • Which structures survived the reported 1953 fire?

This building-by-building approach places individual lives inside the physical station landscape: not only who operated an enterprise, but where its work occurred and how the railway made that work possible.

Family and community knowledge

Descendants and local families may retain photographs, correspondence, tools, stories or knowledge identifying the factory building. Such testimony could clarify Svejkovský’s role during a complicated period, but contributors are not asked to defend or condemn him. The archive’s purpose is to establish what happened from attributable evidence and to correct the record wherever new evidence requires it.

Research questions

  • Was this factory the former Brandeis–Eisenschimmel works, part of it, or a separate enterprise?
  • When did Svejkovský begin operating under his own name?
  • What was manufactured in Kařez between 1939 and 1948?
  • What happened to the machinery, designs, employees and property after nationalisation?
  • Was there a later transfer to Zbirovia or another national enterprise?
