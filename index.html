Stanice Zbiroh Historical Research Archive

The railway station, Borek works, Hotel Borek, workers’ settlement and former Zbiroh estate formed one connected industrial and social landscape. This archive brings their separated histories—and the people behind them—back together.

Why this place matters

The railway reached this landscape before Bethel Henry Strousberg acquired the Zbiroh estate in 1868 and attempted to transform it through industry, transport, housing and supporting institutions. After his collapse, the properties and enterprises fragmented. The Borek works subsequently developed through the Brandeis and Eisenschimmel families, while the station, hotel and surrounding settlement continued to change around them.

The archive reconstructs those changes from maps, company material, correspondence, photographs, postcards, newspapers and public records. It does not force incomplete evidence into a finished story: documented facts, interpretations and unresolved links are identified separately.

Restoring names to industrial history

The history of Borek is not only a history of buildings and machines. The archive gives particular attention to the Brandeis and Eisenschimmel families, whose names belonged to the factory and whose history was fractured by Nazi persecution and the Holocaust. It seeks to restore those people to the account without claiming more than the surviving evidence can prove.

The central questions

  • How did the 1868 purchase and financing of the Zbiroh estate begin the area’s industrial transformation?
  • Which parts of Strousberg’s integrated system were completed and operating before the collapse of 1875?
  • How were the estate, Hotel Borek and factory parcels divided and transferred afterwards?
  • How did the Brandeis–Eisenschimmel enterprise develop, and what was its legal and productive status around 15 March 1939?
  • What do the 1938 and 1946 aerial surveys prove about physical expansion during the occupation—and what remains unknown about its operator and purpose?
