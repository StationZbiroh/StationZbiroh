Catalogue analysis

Evidence status: The following is a working analysis supplied to the archive. It is presented separately from the primary PDF. Individual claims should be checked against the catalogue pages and independent archival sources before being treated as established historical fact.

The document is described as a technical and commercial catalogue for Železárna Borek a továrna hospodářských strojů, akc. spol. (Brandeis–Eisenschimmel), an industrial enterprise operating in Czechoslovakia in the early twentieth century.

Operational mechanics of the catalogue

  • Telegraphic encryption system: the analysis identifies a substantial “Telegraphic Key”, allowing customers to communicate complex queries and orders using compact code words—for example, “Halida” for “Do you have in stock?”—reducing telegram length and potentially reducing language ambiguity.
  • Modular product coding: products use specific marks or model identifiers such as EWA or JS 10", connecting orders to defined technical specifications and replacement requirements.
  • Life-cycle support: the sales conditions are described as covering factory installation, a six-month performance warranty and a standardized repair system.

Industrial scope

The analysis describes a vertically integrated enterprise extending beyond agricultural machinery: foundry work for electrical and chemical-industry castings, machine tools including lathes and drills, and construction equipment including elevators and mixers. It reports foundry capacity for castings of up to five tons.

Industrial and social infrastructure

The analysis interprets the catalogue as evidence of an industrial-colony model, referring to worker housing (Colonies I and II), a factory hotel and administrative/social facilities. If confirmed page-by-page, these references are especially important for reconstructing the relationship between the factory, Hotel Borek, Kařez and Zbiroh station.

Logistical geography

The catalogue analysis identifies Kařez as a postal connection and Zbiroh station as a logistical connection. This should be checked against the catalogue's original address and ordering pages and then cross-referenced with railway records.

Capitalisation and modern valuation

The supplied analysis states that historical records place company capital at approximately 2.6 million Czechoslovak crowns during early-1920s expansion. That figure requires an independent archival citation. The suggested modern corporate valuation of roughly US$50–150 million or more is an interpretive comparison, not a historical valuation, and is therefore not treated by this archive as an established fact.

Technical leads for further verification

The analysis identifies possible patented or branded products including “High-Speed Elevators” and “Cyklon” exhaust cutters, as well as precision castings for electrical machinery such as motors and dynamos. These provide useful search terms for patent registers, trade catalogues and industrial journals.
