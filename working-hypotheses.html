Working hypotheses

Possible explanations are stated clearly so that documents, photographs and physical evidence can test—and, where necessary, disprove—them.

How this page works

A hypothesis is not presented as a historical fact. Each entry records the proposed explanation, the observations that prompted it, evidence that would strengthen it, and evidence that could disprove or substantially revise it. Failed hypotheses remain useful because they narrow the archive’s questions.

Open hypothesis · architectural

Did Brandeis create an upper-storey apartment at Hotel Borek?

The factory-catalogue image and the present factory-facing elevation appear to differ in their upper-storey treatment. Brandeis may have enlarged or reorganised the upper part of the hotel as his apartment, providing a direct outlook toward the factory while keeping him close to both the works and Zbiroh station. The projecting lower structure may also have supported a terrace or shared lookout for the proprietor and hotel guests.

What presently supports it

  • The local account says Brandeis added a staircase and converted guest rooms into his apartment.
  • The present elevation has three visible window levels and faces the factory.
  • The catalogue and present building appear architecturally different.

What could disprove or revise it

  • A dated plan showing the uppermost level existed before Brandeis.
  • The original Czech record locating the apartment conclusively on the first storey above ground.
  • Evidence that the projecting structure had no terrace or viewing function.

Dating consequence: if independent records show that the relevant alteration was complete by 1922, the catalogue image could predate 1922. The images alone do not prove that date. See the architectural comparison.

Open hypothesis · institutional

Did the 1953 factory fire lead to the hotel’s institutional separation?

Hotel Borek may have remained functionally connected to the Borek factory through the Brandeis era and under a post-war successor organisation. After the reported factory fire in 1953, the remaining assets may have been reorganised. A production organisation could not readily turn the hotel into a factory, but the building could continue as accommodation, offices, storage or institutional premises; this may explain its reported transfer to Hospodářské skladištní a výrobní družstvo Plzeň.

What presently supports it

  • The catalogue explicitly describes a factory hotel and officials’ social house.
  • The hotel, factory and station formed one closely connected landscape.
  • The reported fire and reported hotel nationalisation or transfer share the year 1953.

What could disprove or revise it

  • A deed showing the hotel had become legally separate much earlier.
  • Evidence that the cooperative controlled it before the fire.
  • Records showing the transfer arose from an unrelated nationalisation process.
  • Proof that factory operations continued without the proposed institutional break.

Next decisive evidence: property-transfer documents, national-administration files, cooperative records, fire reports and records identifying the factory’s legal successor immediately before and after 1953.
