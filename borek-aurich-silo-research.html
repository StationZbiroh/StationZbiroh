Borek, Richard Aurich and the silo question

Surviving Železárna Borek literature documents the promotion of silo equipment for preserving green fodder. The material opens a new line of research into Richard Aurich's technology, Czechoslovak patent rights, and whether Borek's agricultural-machinery activity helps explain the later agricultural storage use of the former factory site.

What the Borek documents establish

Archive photographs show printed material headed Železárna Borek a továrna hospodářských strojů, akc. spol. The material discusses the advantages of storing green fodder and illustrates a press for a silo for preserving green fodder. It associates the system with the name Aurich and contains patent-related language. These sheets demonstrate Borek's commercial interest in silo and agricultural-storage technology; they do not by themselves establish the exact publication year or the precise legal ownership of the underlying patent rights.

1927–1937 register volume
Official archive
Silo- und Kulturtechnik Richard Aurich, Dresden appears in the Dresden commercial register as HR 20690, transferred from the earlier register A 3537. This independently places Aurich's silo business in the interwar commercial record.

The patent question

The next task is not simply to ask whether Borek “bought a patent.” Several legal arrangements are possible: Borek might have owned a Czechoslovak patent, acquired an assignment, held a territorial licence, manufactured under licence, or acted as a sales or production representative. Until the patent file or contractual evidence is found, none of these possibilities should be presented as established.

  1. What Czechoslovak patent or patent application corresponds to the Aurich silo/press shown in the Borek literature?
  2. Who was named as applicant and inventor?
  3. Was the right originally held by Richard Aurich or his Dresden business?
  4. Was any Czechoslovak right assigned to Železárna Borek, or did Borek manufacture under licence?
  5. When did Borek obtain the right to manufacture or market the system?
  6. Can the patent filing, grant, assignment or renewal history date the surviving Borek sheets more precisely?

Could the silo technology explain the later use of the Borek site?

A large grain-storage silo later existed on the former factory site. That physical fact should not yet be treated as proof of continuity with the interwar Aurich system: the surviving Borek literature concerns the preservation of green fodder, while later grain storage may represent a different technology and a later institutional use.

Nevertheless, the connection is worth testing. Borek was demonstrably engaged with agricultural machinery and silo technology. The research question is therefore: did the site's later agricultural-storage function develop out of an existing Borek specialisation, or was the former industrial complex independently repurposed for grain storage only after manufacturing had ceased?

Why the date matters

A later local history gives a cessation date for Borek production, but the archive has not yet located a primary liquidation or dissolution record establishing the legal end of the company. The Aurich material therefore needs to be dated independently. If the Borek silo literature proves to post-date the claimed cessation of production, it would be evidence requiring the chronology to be reconsidered. It might indicate continuing manufacture, a narrower agricultural-machinery operation, licensing activity, reorganisation, or another form of commercial continuity. The evidence does not yet tell us which.

Research target

Locate the Czechoslovak patent file. It may provide the cleanest bridge between the physical Borek documents and an exact date. The ideal record would identify the invention, inventor, applicant, filing date, patent owner, Czechoslovak representative and any later assignment or licensing history.
