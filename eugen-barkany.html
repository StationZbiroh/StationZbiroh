Eugen Bárkány (1885–1967)

Artist, collector and co-founder of the Jewish Museum in Prešov (Eperjes).

Overview

Eugen Bárkány was instrumental in establishing the Jewish Museum in Prešov, together with Dr. Teodor (Tivadar) Austerlitz. The museum association was established in 1928; the museum opened to the public in 1931. The museum documented the material culture and history of the region's Jewish communities.

Documented and published chronology

  • 1928: Establishment of the Jewish Museum Association in Prešov.
  • 1931: Public opening of the museum.
  • 1939: The museum closed under wartime conditions; published historical research describes confiscation and safeguarding of much of the collection.
  • After 1945: The museum was not re-established in its former form.
  • 1950: Date inscribed on one portrait attributed to Bárkány.
  • 1951: A published biographical account reports that Bárkány and his wife were forcibly displaced from Prešov as politically unreliable. Their subsequent places of residence included Meretice and Smolník.
  • 1952: The Prešov museum collection was transferred to the State Jewish Museum in Prague. A published study reports that the head of Bezalel in Jerusalem had expressed interest in transferring the collection there, but the Czechoslovak state refused. The study does not establish the precise year of that expression of interest.
  • 1953: Date inscribed on a second portrait attributed to Bárkány, identifying its subject as a former mayor of Meretice.
  • 1955: Bárkány settled in Bratislava, according to the published biographical account.
  • 1993: The museum collection returned to Prešov.

Two portraits: current research record

Two portraits attributed to Bárkány, dated 1950 and 1953, were acquired by a private collector at the Kolbenova flea market in Prague. Their ownership history before that acquisition has not been established. The following inscriptions were read by Dr. Pnina Younger of the National Library of Israel in correspondence dated 14 October 2026:

  • 1950 portrait: “B. Führmann / 1950 / Bárkány”. The identity of B. Führmann is unconfirmed.
  • 1953 portrait: “Bývalý richtar v Mereticiach” (“Former mayor of Meretice”). The sitter's identity is unconfirmed.

Portrait photographs and inscription images will be added here after upload. The dates and inscriptions are recorded separately from unverified sitter identifications.

Bezalel and the collection

A published historical account reports that the head of Bezalel in Jerusalem expressed interest in acquiring the Prešov museum collection, but the Czechoslovak state refused its transfer. The available passage does not identify the Bezalel correspondent or date the proposal. It does not establish that Bárkány personally corresponded with Bezalel, nor does it establish a connection between the proposal and his displacement.

Historical context and hypothesis

Working hypothesis — not established fact.

The reported 1951 displacement may relate to the communist-era removal of people classified as politically unreliable, discussed in historical scholarship under the heading Action B (Akcia B). The precise administrative basis and timing of Bárkány's removal remain to be verified from contemporary records. Whether contacts with institutions or individuals in Israel played any role is unknown.

Questions requiring evidence

Who were the portrait sitters? When and through whom did Bezalel express interest in the collection? Who were the surviving members of the museum association who emigrated to Israel? What contemporary records explain Bárkány's displacement and the collection's transfer?
