From Mladá Boleslav to America: the family and name-change trail

Research update · 3 October 2026

The municipal cultural history Eisenschimmelova vila 2 identifies the Eisenschimmels as a Jewish family from Mladá Boleslav. It reports that Franz and his wife Rosalie emigrated in 1914 and became American citizens in 1926. It separately identifies Franz's brother Johann as John Robert, married to Dorothy and settled in Chicago, with the same reported emigration and citizenship years. These are secondary biographical statements; the underlying passenger, citizenship and name-change records have not yet been located by this archive.

The local history places the brothers' adoption of Robert after the creation of Czechoslovakia, but supplies no legal date or file reference. It proposes possible motives rather than documenting their reasons. Since Johann had already emigrated in 1914, we need to determine whether his change was recorded in America, Czechoslovakia or both. Jan Robert is a search variant, not a verified name used by Johann. No primary name-change order or securely matched American business record was found in the additional web searches on 3 October 2026.

Practical routes to the original evidence

  • Identify the source behind the biography. Ask the Mladá Boleslav cultural-history publisher to identify the author and citations for the 1914 departure, 1926 citizenship and Robert surname. Published contact: infocentrum@kulturamb.cz.
  • Reconstruct Czech identity. Use the National Archives' Jewish-register portal to seek birth and family records, including any later annotations. Ask SOkA Mladá Boleslav about surviving residence, municipal and district-administration records or the correct repository for surname-change files. Published contact: soka-mlada.boleslav@soapraha.cz. These are research routes, not confirmed family-file holdings.
  • Follow Johann into Chicago. The National Archives' naturalisation guide and Northern Illinois naturalisation index, 1926–1979 are starting points. Search both surnames and match birthplace, relatives and spouse before accepting a result. Chicago is established for Johann by the local history, not yet for Franz.
  • Locate family and business papers. Search the Leo Baeck Institute catalogue for Eisenschimmel, Robert and associated relatives. Its collections include correspondence, genealogy and business records. No specific Eisenschimmel family collection or university study establishing the American company trail has yet been identified.

The industrial question: surviving correspondence, shareholder lists, probate inventories, patent assignments or transfer contracts could establish whether emigrant relatives retained shares, transferred rights or continued a business in America. Emigration and a surname change alone do not demonstrate a transfer of production or ownership. No American production transfer is asserted here.
