Borek Airfield

A working historical timeline of the former Czechoslovak military field airfield at Borek, near Kařez and Zbiroh. The surviving evidence connects the site with military aviation, the Brdy training area and the mobilisation of 1938, while its wartime fate and land ownership remain open research questions.

Research note: this page separates documented military use from unresolved questions. References to private ownership of land should not be read as evidence that Borek was a private civilian airfield. Land ownership, military control and military use are separate questions.

c. 1930

A field airfield at Borek

Military-history research records the establishment of the Borek field airfield around 1930. It was used mainly for cooperation training between aviation and artillery connected with the nearby Brdy training and firing area.

Source: Borek, Polní letiště — Valka.cz · cited there: Jan Michl, “Polní letiště Borek”, Military revue 2023/08.

12 July 1932

Aero Ap-11.54 accident

An Aero Ap-11.54 (D7) of Letka 2, Letecký pluk 1 crashed at Borek. The published account preserves conflicting information about the pilot, so the identity is left unresolved here.

Source and photograph: Valka.cz

1934

Military stores and the ownership question

Research material refers to aviation fuel and military material at Borek and also contains a reference connecting František Racek with ownership of the airfield site. The precise legal ownership, parcels and boundaries have not yet been independently reconstructed from cadastral records.

Status: documentary lead · cadastral verification still required.

1935

The Cierva C.30A question

A secondary source captions a Cierva C-30A (S8 / Avro 671) as being tested at Borek. Correspondence during this archive's research, however, indicated that testing took place at the Military Technical and Aviation Institute at Praha-Letňany. The exact Borek connection therefore remains unresolved.

Published Borek attribution: Valka.cz

1938

The airfield in the mobilisation year

The 1938 aerial photograph shows a developed field airfield with numerous aircraft dispersed around the site. Several appear visually similar, consistent with operation by an organised military unit, although aircraft type should not be identified from the photograph alone.

Independent mobilisation records confirm that Letka 4 was deployed at Borek and equipped with Letov Š-328 observation aircraft. The unit was at Borek from 24 September to 22 October 1938 under Captain Jan Bílek.

Letka 4 record — Valka.cz · Czechoslovak Air Force order of battle, 30 September 1938 · 1938 ČÚZK aerial survey

1939–1945

Occupation and an unresolved disappearance

The wartime use of the field remains uncertain. A comparison of the surviving pre-war and post-war aerial photography appears to indicate that structures visible around the airfield before the war were no longer present after it. This establishes a question, not a cause: the date and circumstances of their removal have not yet been documented.

Open question: was the field abandoned, dismantled, repurposed or returned to agriculture during the occupation?

1941

The Anthropoid question

Published historical material has associated a Borek airfield with planning surrounding Operation Anthropoid. Because the identification and precise role of the Borek site require further verification, the connection is retained here as a research question rather than a settled event in the airfield chronology.

Status: secondary-source lead · further archival verification required.

1946

Post-war aerial evidence

The 1946 state aerial survey provides a direct comparison with the 1938 landscape and is central to investigating the disappearance or alteration of the airfield infrastructure.

Open the 1946 ČÚZK aerial survey

1951

The former airfield landscape

A further state aerial survey records the site in 1951. Read together, the 1938, 1946 and 1951 images provide a visual sequence for tracing the transformation of the former airfield.
