Factory Catalogue

A surviving technical and commercial catalogue associated with Železárna Borek a továrna hospodářských strojů, akc. spol. (Brandeis–Eisenschimmel), preserved here as a central source for reconstructing the industrial complex and its commercial network.

The catalogue has been described in the research correspondence as dating approximately to the 1920s–1930s. Its exact title, issue date and pagination should be recorded directly from the document as the source is catalogued in detail.

Independent historical context for the catalogue

The catalogue is best understood alongside independent contemporary records of the company’s formation and market position.

1919 company listing

A 1919 Czech official publication lists the enterprise as Akciová slévárna železa a továrna na stroje ku vyrábění nástrojů Brandeis, Eisenschimmel, Borek v Čechách. This is useful independent evidence that the enterprise was operating in corporate form and being recorded among industrial companies of the period.

Open the 1919 Czech source

Conversion into a joint-stock company

A contemporary German technical publication reports the combination of E. Brandeis’s Borek ironworks with Franz Eisenschimmel & Co.’s agricultural-machinery factory in Roudnice nad Labem into a joint-stock company. This provides particularly important context for why the surviving catalogue covers such a broad industrial range.

Open the contemporary German technical publication

Capitalisation: conflicting figures

The German technical source gives a capital figure of approximately 2.4 million crowns. The earlier Gemini catalogue analysis cited 2.6 million Czechoslovak crowns. Because these figures do not agree, this archive records the discrepancy rather than selecting one without further documentary support.

Longer industrial chronology

A secondary historical account of Strousberg and the Borek works provides a longer narrative from the nineteenth-century works through later ownership, Brandeis, the 1920 corporate combination and the Depression-era decline. It is useful as a research guide, but individual dates and claims should be checked against archival records.

Working analysis of the catalogue

Evidence status: This analysis is separate from the primary PDF. Individual claims should be checked against the original catalogue pages and, where appropriate, independent archival sources before being promoted to established historical fact.

The catalogue functions as more than a promotional brochure. It appears to operate as a technical and commercial interface for remote ordering and industrial supply.

Telegraphic ordering system

A substantial section is described as a telegraphic key, allowing customers to communicate complex questions and orders with compact code words. One example given in the analysis is “Halida” for “Do you have in stock?”. Such systems reduced telegram length and could also reduce ambiguity across languages.

Product coding and standardisation

Products are identified by specific marks or model codes, including examples such as EWA or JS 10". These codes linked commercial orders to technical specifications and replacement requirements, suggesting standardised series production.

Installation, warranty and repair

The catalogue analysis reports sales conditions covering factory installation, a six-month performance warranty and a standardised repair system, indicating support across the product life cycle rather than simple point-of-sale transactions.

Industrial scope

The enterprise is described as extending well beyond agricultural machinery. The analysis identifies foundry work for electrical and chemical-industry castings, machine tools such as lathes and drills, and construction equipment including elevators and mixers. It also reports a foundry capacity for castings of up to five tons.

Industrial and social infrastructure

The analysis identifies references to worker housing known as Colonies I and II, a factory hotel and administrative or social facilities. If confirmed page by page, this material is important evidence for reconstructing the wider industrial settlement around Borek/Kařez rather than viewing the factory as an isolated production site.

Railway and postal connections

The analysis identifies Kařez as a postal connection and Zbiroh station as a logistical connection. These references are particularly important to the central research question of how the industrial complex interacted with the railway and should be cross-checked against the catalogue's original ordering/address pages and railway records.

Capitalisation and valuation

The earlier analysis stated that the company was capitalised at approximately 2.6 million Czechoslovak crowns. A contemporary German technical source located during subsequent research gives approximately 2.4 million crowns. The discrepancy remains open pending verification from company registers or other primary corporate records. The suggested modern comparison of US$50–150 million or more remains interpretive only and is not treated as a historical valuation.

Technical research leads

The analysis identifies possible patented or branded products including “High-Speed Elevators” and “Cyklon” exhaust cutters, as well as precision castings for electrical machinery such as motors and dynamos. These are useful leads for patent registers, industrial journals and additional catalogues.

Analysis attribution: Gemini. The analysis has been preserved as a research aid and is clearly separated from the primary document and from independently verified archival conclusions.

Nezávislý historický kontext

Český pramen z roku 1919 uvádí podnik mezi průmyslovými akciovými společnostmi. Dobový německý technický tisk dále popisuje spojení železáren E. Brandeise v Borku s továrnou zemědělských strojů Franz Eisenschimmel & Co. v Roudnici nad Labem do akciové společnosti. Německý pramen uvádí kapitál přibližně 2,4 milionu korun, zatímco dřívější analýza Gemini uváděla 2,6 milionu; rozdíl je proto ponechán jako nevyřešený.

Pramen z roku 1919 · Dobový německý technický pramen · Historický přehled Borek/Strousberg

Pracovní analýza katalogu

Unabhängiger historischer Kontext

Eine tschechische Quelle von 1919 führt das Unternehmen unter den industriellen Aktiengesellschaften. Eine zeitgenössische deutsche technische Publikation beschreibt anschließend die Verbindung der Boreker Eisenwerke von E. Brandeis mit der Landmaschinenfabrik Franz Eisenschimmel & Co. in Roudnice nad Labem zu einer Aktiengesellschaft. Die deutsche Quelle nennt etwa 2,4 Millionen Kronen Kapital, während die frühere Gemini-Analyse 2,6 Millionen nannte; die Abweichung bleibt daher ausdrücklich offen.
