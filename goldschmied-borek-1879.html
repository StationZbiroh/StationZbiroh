Goldschmied, Roubíček and Borek: the 1879 evidence

This page records a potentially important bridge between the collapse of Strousberg’s industrial system and the later appearance of Roubíček and Brandeis at the Borek works. It separates what the surviving evidence establishes from the questions that remain open.

← Back to Who was Emil Brandeis?

What the archive currently establishes

The archive holds research material based on an original 1879 land-title/property map for land near Zbiroh station. The record names Emil Goldschmied, Ludwig Goldschmied and Jakob Roubicek. Family research identifies Emil and Ludwig as sons of Abraham Goldschmied and Theresia Goldschmied. The same research places Emil at approximately 16 years old in 1879 and gives Ludwig a birth year of 1855.

These facts do not by themselves establish why Jakob Roubicek appears with the brothers, whether he was a lawyer, guardian, creditor, co-owner, administrator or other representative, or whether every parcel shown in the 1879 material formed part of the Borek factory itself. Those questions remain under investigation.

Working chronology

1875
Established context
Strousberg’s financial system collapses.
The subsequent ownership and administration of the Borek works is not yet completely reconstructed.
1879
Primary property evidence
Goldschmied and Roubicek names appear in the property material.
The archive’s 1879 land-title research records Emil Goldschmied, Ludwig Goldschmied and Jakob Roubicek in connection with property near Zbiroh station. Emil was approximately 16; Ludwig, born in 1855, was approximately 23–24 depending on the exact document date. Their ages make the legal structure of the ownership or representation especially worth investigating, but the applicable historical rules of legal capacity still need to be established from period law.
1881
Secondary industrial history
Roubíček and Brandeis are recorded as acquiring the Borek works.
This later appearance of the Roubíček name makes the 1879 record potentially significant. It raises the question of whether Roubíček’s earlier role was connected with the later transfer into the Brandeis period.
1887
Secondary industrial history
E. Brandeis is recorded as buying Roubíček’s share.
The transaction is a further reason to investigate exactly what legal or commercial interest Roubíček held between 1879 and 1887.

Goldschmied family evidence

A genealogy screenshot held by the archive identifies Abraham Goldschmied, born 23 February 1819 in Náchod, as husband of Theresia Goldschmied and father of Daniel Goldschmied, the Goldschmied twins, Ludwig Goldschmied, Moses Moritz Goldschmied and Emil Goldschmied. This family structure is useful because it establishes that Emil and Ludwig were brothers rather than unrelated people sharing a surname.

The genealogy should be treated as supporting family-history evidence. The original property record remains the primary source for the 1879 property connection.

Questions now created by the evidence

  1. What exact parcels and buildings are covered by the 1879 Goldschmied–Roubicek entry?
  2. What legal interest did Emil and Ludwig Goldschmied each hold?
  3. What was Jakob Roubicek’s occupation and formal role?
  4. Did Roubicek act for himself, for the Goldschmied family, for creditors, or in another representative capacity?
  5. What legal-capacity rules applied to Emil and Ludwig in Bohemia in 1879?
  6. How, if at all, did the 1879 arrangement lead to the documented Roubíček–Brandeis acquisition in 1881?
  7. What exactly did E. Brandeis buy from Roubíček in 1887?

Interpretive caution

A possible chain can now be investigated: Strousberg collapse → Goldschmied/Roubicek property evidence in 1879 → Roubíček and Brandeis in 1881 → Brandeis buys Roubíček’s share in 1887. This is a research sequence, not yet a proven chain of title. The archive will keep sale, guardianship, legal representation, family connection, creditor involvement and other mechanisms open until supported by documentary evidence.
