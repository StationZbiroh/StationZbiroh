Borek factory

A consolidated chronology of the Borek industrial site. This page brings together events previously scattered across the station, catalogue, Brandeis, Eisenschimmel, airfield and property pages while leaving detailed evidence on those source pages.

Evidence rule: this timeline does not treat continuity of a product name as proof that production continued at the physical Borek site. Likewise, aerial change proves physical change, not the operator or purpose of new buildings.

Industrial-topography research records a 1918 agreement to transfer agricultural-machinery production from Roudnice nad Labem to Borek. A 1919 Czech listing and a contemporary German technical publication document the combined company.

1931–1934

Foundry operation ends; the site remains in use

Local and heritage accounts place the end of foundry activity around 1931–1932. A February 1934 account describes ironworks buildings as out of operation. Separate historical material reports subsequent grain-storage use. These facts should be kept distinct from the later history of Borek-derived products.

New discovery · 3 October 2026

From Roudnice to Borek: the Eisenschimmel connection

An illustrated advertisement in the Tiroler Land-Zeitung, 18 September 1909, identifies Franz Eisenschimmel & Co., Raudnitz an der Elbe (Roudnice nad Labem) as a manufacturer of agricultural machinery and a foundry. Its central sales office names Franz Melichar and Rudolf Bächer, at Löwengasse 37, Vienna III/2. The advertisement has now been checked against a user-supplied image of the digitised newspaper.

The advertised range includes wine and fruit presses, grape and fruit mills, fodder-processing machinery, chaff cutters, beet cutters and grain crushers. It documents a commercial sales connection between the named firms in 1909; it does not establish joint ownership. No silo is advertised here. Original newspaper source, ULB Tirol.

Strong secondary evidence: ČVUT's industrial-heritage publication Co jsme si zbořili, entry “Franz Eisenschimmel a spol., továrna na stroje a slévárna,” records a factory established in 1905 in Pracnerova Street, Roudnice. It describes three three-aisled halls and an engine house with boiler house, and says the buildings became part of neighbouring Rudolf Bächer's works from 1918. The entry includes a historical factory illustration dated 1910. ČVUT publication and illustrations.

ČVUT's separate Borek entry reports a 1918 agreement with Franz Eisenschimmel & Co. to transfer agricultural-machinery production, followed by machinery being brought from Roudnice to Borek within the combined joint-stock enterprise. Industrial Topography: Strousbergova železárna.

What remains to be documented: the original transfer contract and commercial-register files are still required to establish which assets, designs, patents and liabilities changed hands. The advertisement names the manufacturer and its sales office; it does not identify an individual engineer or inventor.

Further primary-source lead: indexed text in the January 1919 Prager Tagblatt reports constitution of a company bearing the Borek, Brandeis and Eisenschimmel names and a takeover of the two businesses. Its page image, exact issue date and complete wording remain to be checked. January 1919 newspaper page.
