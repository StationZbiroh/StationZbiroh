Borek factory

A consolidated chronology of the Borek industrial site. This page brings together events previously scattered across the station, catalogue, Brandeis, Eisenschimmel, airfield and property pages while leaving detailed evidence on those source pages.

Evidence rule: this timeline does not treat continuity of a product name as proof that production continued at the physical Borek site. Likewise, aerial change proves physical change, not the operator or purpose of new buildings.
1875–1881

Post-Strousberg industrial property is redistributed

The collapse of the Strousberg system begins the ownership sequence from which the later Borek works emerges. The precise parcel-by-parcel chain remains a continuing land-register research task.

Ownership reconstruction Property research · Strousberg context

1881

Brandeis enters the Borek ownership story

The archive’s current chronology places the transfer from Mančík and Šlesinger to Roubíček and Brandeis in 1881. Primary title and company records remain the preferred evidence for refining the transaction.

Chronology established · primary title evidence still sought Brandeis chronology · Ownership chain

1887

Brandeis control develops

The Brandeis biographical research records a later stage in which Emil Brandeis acquired the other share. The identity of individual Brandeis family members must continue to be checked against company and family records.

Identity and company records being reconciled Emil Brandeis

1918–c. 1920

Brandeis and Eisenschimmel enterprises combine at Borek

Industrial-topography research records a 1918 agreement to transfer agricultural-machinery production from Roudnice nad Labem to Borek. A 1919 Czech listing and a contemporary German technical publication document the combined company.

Documented corporate transition Franz Eisenschimmel · Company context

1920s–1930s

The surviving catalogue records a broad industrial system

The catalogue documents machinery, commercial systems, social infrastructure and transport references. Its exact issue date and page-level catalogue are still being refined, so it should not be used to force a more precise chronology than the object permits.

Exact catalogue date unresolved Factory catalogue · Silo documents

1931–1934

Foundry operation ends; the site remains in use

Local and heritage accounts place the end of foundry activity around 1931–1932. A February 1934 account describes ironworks buildings as out of operation. Separate historical material reports subsequent grain-storage use. These facts should be kept distinct from the later history of Borek-derived products.

Secondary/local evidence · property records still needed Master chronology · Ownership questions

1932–1941

Borek-derived products continue beyond reported site closure

Melichar catalogue and photographic material traces Borek-derived agricultural machines through the 1930s and into 1941. This supports product-line continuity elsewhere; it does not establish continued production at the physical Borek works.

Product continuity documented · production location unresolved Catalogue sequence and context

15 March 1939

Legal status at the beginning of occupation remains unresolved

The surviving commercial material does not yet establish whether the Brandeis–Eisenschimmel company was operating, in liquidation or surviving only as a residual legal entity. Commercial-register and liquidation files are required.

Primary legal record sought Research register · Property and ownership research

30 July 1940

The full Brandeis–Eisenschimmel name remains in public circulation

A museum-catalogued Borek item was sent on this date under the full company name. It proves circulation of the name and image after occupation began, but an older printed item could have been posted later and therefore does not prove production or ownership in 1940.

Dated museum catalogue record Factory images and record

1938–1946

The physical factory footprint becomes substantially larger

Comparison of state aerial surveys shows additional structures on land that appears open in 1938. Most of the interval falls within the occupation, making the change historically significant. The photographs do not identify the buildings’ exact construction dates, uses, operator or products.

Post-war

Ownership, reuse and nationalisation require a separate chain

The post-war fate of the factory land should be reconstructed from title, confiscation, nationalisation and operational records rather than inferred from the history of neighbouring Hotel Borek or from later agricultural storage.
