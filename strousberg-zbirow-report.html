Bethel Henry Strousberg and the Zbirow Industrial Project

A contemporary 1875 engineering and financial assessment of the Zbirow industrial system, preserved at the end of Bethel Henry Strousberg's 1876 memoir.

Source context. Strousberg's Dr. Strousberg und sein Wirken: von ihm selbst geschildert was published in Berlin in 1876. The 486-page volume ends with the appendix Herrschaft Zbirow und deren Rentabilität, beginning on p. 482. The report reproduced here is addressed to H. v. Goldschmidt & Co. in Vienna; its concluding table is signed by Ržiha, k.k. Ober-Ingenieur, Vienna, 1 March 1875.

Historical introduction

Bethel Henry Strousberg (1823–1884) developed railway and industrial enterprises across nineteenth-century Central and Eastern Europe. His Zbirow undertaking was conceived not as a single isolated factory but as an interconnected industrial system built around mineral resources, forests, iron production, engineering and rail transport.

The surviving report is unusually valuable because it describes that system while it was still being assessed as a working economic project. It connects iron-ore and coal mining, coke production, blast furnaces, rolling mills, steam sawmills, wagon manufacture, workers' and officials' housing, and industrial railways. Among the places named are Borek, Holoubkov, Dobřív, Strašice, Zbirow and Bubna near Prague.

The timing is important. The assessment is dated March 1875, close to Strousberg's financial collapse later that year. The concluding pages nevertheless describe extensive mineral reserves, productive capacity and substantial projected profitability; they do not present exhaustion of the iron ore or physical failure of the Zbirow works as the explanation for his financial difficulties. This makes it important to distinguish the collapse of Strousberg's finances from the subsequent fate and economic value of the industrial assets themselves.

This archive deliberately separates source, translation and interpretation. The original page is presented first. Translation follows it. Historical interpretation is kept separate so that readers can inspect the evidence themselves.

Page 482 — English translation

Report to Messrs. H. v. Goldschmidt & Co. in Vienna concerning the industrial establishments of Dr. Strousberg situated on the Zbirow Estate.

I. Foundations and general description of the industrial works.

The industrial works on the Zbirow Estate are based upon the concentrated occurrence of considerable quantities of raw materials. Within the enclosed territory of the Zbirow Estate, comprising approximately 5½ square miles, as well as immediately adjacent to its boundaries, Dr. Strousberg possesses approximately 376 million centners of iron ore, 115 million centners of coal, and 37,865 Joch of forest.

Page 483 — English translation

The Zbirow industrial works were established for the exploitation of these raw materials, with the iron ores forming their foundation. The ores were to be smelted using wood, coke produced from coal of the local mining district, and limestone from the Zbirow region, producing cast goods, rolled iron and steel goods, partly for sale and partly for further processing.

The various works for extracting and refining the raw materials were connected with one another by standard-gauge railways, enabling rapid and economical transport between the works and connection with the principal railway lines crossing the Zbirow region.

The undertaking required a considerable body of officials and workers, and extensive facilities and housing were provided. The report groups the installations as iron-ore mining, coal mining, coke ovens, blast furnaces, rolling mills, steam sawmills, wagon factory, officials' and workers' housing, and industrial railways. Limestone quarries, clay pits and brickworks are treated as auxiliary installations.

Page 484 — English translation

The iron mines distributed through the estate and immediately beyond its boundaries supplied ores to the blast furnaces. The smelted iron was used either directly for castings or converted into pig iron for further processing.

For this purpose rolling mills had initially been established at Dobřiv near Strašice and at Borek near Zbirow. Their products were intended partly for sale and partly for further processing in the puddling furnaces, for a planned bridge-construction establishment, for the existing wagon factory at Holoubkau, and for the wagon factory at Bubna near Prague.

The wagon factories drew upon the timber resources of the Zbirow estate and used sawn timber produced by the steam sawmills at Zbirow and Holoubkau. Surplus sawn timber was exported to Bavaria and Saxony and supplied to domestic demand.

Page 485 — English translation

The report sets out the projected scale of the system: millions of centners of iron ore and coal, large quantities of pig iron, cast and rolled goods, thousands of railway wagons, coke production, steam-sawn timber, and very substantial internal freight carried by the industrial railway.

It states that the housing installations could accommodate approximately 5,000 people. The report presents the concentration of these activities within the Zbirow area, together with their railway connections, as capable of substantial economic importance.

VI. Résumé. The report then turns from the physical industrial system to its capital requirements and expected profitability.

Page 486 — financial conclusion

The final page presents the capital values and projected profitability of the individual components of the Zbirow industrial undertaking. It treats the mines, coke works, blast furnaces, rolling mills, sawmills, wagon works, housing and industrial railways as parts of one financial system.

The table gives total capital on the order of 19.5 million guilders and projected annual net income exceeding 4 million guilders, corresponding to an overall projected return of approximately 21 percent.

The assessment is dated Vienna, 1 March 1875 and signed Ržiha, k.k. Ober-Ingenieur.

Historical interpretation — starting point

The document changes the scale at which the local industrial history can be examined. Borek appears not as an isolated works but as one component of a larger system linking mines, furnaces, rolling mills, forests, sawmills, wagon manufacture and railway infrastructure. The explicit connection to Holoubkov and Bubna near Prague also raises questions about how production, finance and ownership were distributed across the wider network.

At the same time, this is an assessment of projected resources, output and profitability. Its figures should therefore be tested against independent mining, company, land-register, bankruptcy and production records rather than treated automatically as achieved results. Later interpretations of the Zbirow project's failure can then be compared directly with this contemporary 1875 account.

Borek factory parcels — 1879 cadastral evidence

Four years after the 1875 engineering assessment, the cadastral documentation for Kařez provides a separate geographical source for investigating the land and ownership history of the historic Borek factory complex itself. Handwritten annotations on the Borek factory property

The three handwritten entries are potentially important because they occur on the mapped property of the historic Borek industrial works. They are therefore not simply names appearing somewhere in the surrounding cadastral district. Their placement makes them directly relevant to reconstructing the ownership or legal history of the factory after the Strousberg period.

Rather than silently modernising difficult nineteenth-century handwriting, the archive will preserve the image and add the three exact names once each reading has been checked against the original cadastral sheet and, ideally, the corresponding land-register documentation. The entries may represent owners, co-owners, creditors, successors or another legal relationship to the factory property.

Research question. The appearance of these names on the Borek factory parcels is particularly significant when considered alongside the 1875 engineering report prepared for H. v. Goldschmidt & Co. in Vienna. One working hypothesis is that interests associated with Strousberg's creditors, financiers or their families may subsequently have held parts of the Borek works. This remains a hypothesis until the names and corresponding land-register entries are independently verified.

Working hypothesis — Goldschmidt, the Borek factory parcels and later ownership

The following interpretation is deliberately presented with some latitude as a working historical hypothesis. It is not being asserted as a proven genealogy or chain of title.

The 1875 report was expressly prepared for H. v. Goldschmidt & Co. in Vienna. That fact places the firm directly within the documentary history of the Zbirow industrial assets at the moment when Strousberg's financial position was deteriorating. The report itself describes an integrated industrial estate with enormous stated mineral resources, extensive works and a projected overall return of about 21 percent. Significantly, it does not explain the crisis through exhausted iron deposits or the disappearance of the underlying industrial resources.

The 1879 Kařez cadastral material then presents three handwritten names directly associated with the mapped parcels of the Borek factory complex. This makes the sequence particularly interesting. One plausible possibility is that former Strousberg industrial property was divided or transferred among creditors, investors, purchasers or members of interconnected families after the collapse. If repeated surnames or family relationships can be demonstrated, changes of recorded name could reflect inheritance, marriage, co-ownership or restructuring rather than wholly unrelated changes of ownership.

This possibility becomes especially interesting when compared with the later history of the Borek works and the appearance of the Brandeis name, including Emil Brandeis. At present there is no demonstrated documentary bridge between Goldschmidt, the three cadastral names and Brandeis. Emil Brandeis's identity and date of birth also need to be matched carefully to the correct archival person before they are used in this chain. The next stage of the research will therefore compare the names and dates against land registers, company records, bankruptcy files, genealogical material and Jewish archival sources.

The hypothesis is consequently stronger than a simple coincidence but remains open to falsification: the financial collapse of Strousberg may have redistributed a valuable functioning industrial estate into the hands of creditors and related interests, with portions of the Borek factory subsequently appearing under individual or family names. The cadastral and company records should allow this proposition either to be demonstrated or rejected.
