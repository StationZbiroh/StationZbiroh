Chronological synthesis

Connected historical timeline

The station, factory, hotel, people and property records belong to overlapping histories. This timeline places their documented points beside one another without filling the gaps between them.

How to read this page: proximity in time does not prove causation. Where two histories appear to meet, the note identifies the record still needed to demonstrate the connection.

Industrial formation and the first railway

1862

The Prague–Plzeň railway creates the transport context

The line opened in 1862, creating the framework within which the later industrial and settlement histories developed. The precise status of stopping or loading facilities at this particular site in 1862 remains unresolved.

Context established · local arrangement unresolved Station chronology

1868–1869

Strousberg acquires the Zbiroh estate

Historical accounts place the estate in Strousberg’s hands. His industrial system, the railway and the landed estate now enter the same research frame, although the exact acquisition instrument is still sought.

Reported acquisition Strousberg · Property rights

c. 1871

Hotel Borek enters the developing industrial settlement

The hotel chronology places its origins in the Strousberg era. Establishing its precise builder, function and title history requires contemporary business and land records.

Collapse, reporting and redistribution

1 March 1875

The Goldschmidt assessment values the Zbirow industrial system

Chief engineer Franz Rziha’s report for H. v. Goldschmidt & Co. includes the Borek works and documents a proposed refinancing relationship. It does not prove that the bank acquired the factory or that it was related to the later Goldschmied parties.

Primary report Zbirow report · Memoir context · The Economist

28 October 1875

Bankruptcy begins the property problem

The collapse did not necessarily transfer the castle, factory, hotel, railway interests and other assets as one unit. The archive therefore follows separate title chains.

Reported in later accounts Property reconstruction

1876

Strousberg publishes his defence

His memoir is evidence of his intentions and self-representation after collapse. It must be compared with contemporary reporting and independent property records.

Interested testimony Memoir summary · Reputation

1877–1879

Assets and names reappear in new records

Auction reports and 1879 property evidence provide fixed points after the collapse. Emil and Ludwig Goldschmied appear with Jakob Roubíček, creating testable questions about inheritance, succession and whether Roubíček represented one or both brothers because of age or legal capacity. The relationship to the earlier Goldschmidt banking house remains unproven.

Brandeis industry and the new station

1908

A business postcard is signed “E. Brandeis”

The object anchors the Brandeis name locally. Attribution to a particular individual or generation requires comparison with civil, company and residence records.

Documented object Brandeis identity research

1911–1913

The new Zbiroh station is designed, built and enters passenger use

The State Railways Directorate project is dated October 1911; heritage evidence dates construction to 1911–1912, another source gives 1912–1913, and a railway account states that passenger use began in 1913. The original planning files remain essential to explaining the building’s scale and resolving the dating.

Documented range · dating discrepancy retained Station history

1919–c. 1920

The industrial company appears in corporate and technical records

A 1919 Czech listing and a contemporary German technical publication document the company and the combination of the Borek and Eisenschimmel enterprises.

Persecution, war and post-war change

1935–1940

A museum catalogue preserves a Borek factory record

The Museum of Securities catalogues an item under the full company name Železárna Borek a továrna hospodářských strojů, akc. spol. Brandeis–Eisenschimmel. It identifies the subject as “Továrna Borek,” dates the printing approximately to 1935–1940, and records that the item was sent on 30 July 1940. These catalogue dates document the surviving object, but do not by themselves establish that the factory remained operational throughout this period.

1932–late 1930s

Company and ownership questions become less certain

The Brandeis research records corporate decline and unresolved later transitions. Company-register and liquidation files are needed to replace broad chronology with legal and operational detail.

Emil Brandeis is persecuted and deported

The victim record provides a documented personal chronology culminating in deportation to Terezín on 13 July 1942 and subsequently to Treblinka. Linking this person to each industrial record remains a separate evidential task.
