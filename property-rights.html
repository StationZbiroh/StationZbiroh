Property Rights

Who actually owned what? This investigation follows the fragmentation of Bethel Henry Strousberg's landed, industrial and railway-related property system after his collapse in 1875, property by property, and asks what contemporary document proves each transfer.

Research method

Possession is not title. Operating a factory, financing it, leasing it, mortgaging it, owning shares in the company that operates it, and owning the underlying land are different legal relationships. Later possession, restitution or institutional histories are not treated automatically as proof of earlier ownership.

For each property this page separates documented title, reported ownership, mortgages and creditor rights, leases, operating control and unresolved gaps. The objective is not to replace the conventional history with an alternative one, but to reconstruct the chain from contemporary land books, auction records, contracts, mortgage records and corporate files wherever those survive.

Research hypothesis

Unproven hypothesis. Did the collapse of Strousberg's enterprises produce a more complicated redistribution of the Zbiroh estate and associated industrial properties than later ownership histories suggest? What roles were played by mortgage institutions, H. v. Goldschmidt & Co., purchasers, lessees and subsequent property holders?

The archive is also testing whether the later appearance of Emil and Ludwig Goldschmied in Borek/Zbiroh property evidence has any demonstrable relationship to the earlier Goldschmidt banking connection. No relationship between Goldschmidt and Goldschmied is assumed without documentary evidence.

The break-up of the Strousberg system

Contemporary and later accounts agree that Strousberg's enormous system was heavily mortgaged and entered insolvency in 1875. The central question here is what happened next to each component. The landed estate and the industrial works may not have followed the same legal path.

1. Zbiroh Castle and the landed estate

1868–1869
Reported acquisition

Strousberg acquires Zbiroh

Historical accounts place the Zbiroh estate in Strousberg's hands from 1868/69. The exact acquisition instrument should be added to this archive.

28 Oct 1875
Insolvency

Strousberg estate enters bankruptcy proceedings

Later historical accounts record formal bankruptcy over Strousberg's property and very large creditor claims.

5 Apr 1877
Strong secondary evidence

Vienna mortgage institution reported purchaser

August Sedláček's later account states that Zbiroh and attached estates were auctioned to the Vienna mortgage institution for 3,909,000 florins.

27 Feb / 29 Mar 1879
Strong secondary evidence · primary deed sought

Colloredo-Mannsfeld acquisition

Several histories report that Josef Colloredo-Mannsfeld acquired Zbiroh and attached estates for 3,350,100 florins. The archive is seeking the original auction adjudication, conveyance and land-book entry to establish exactly what property was included.

Open questions: What parcels and rights constituted the “Zbiroh estate” in the 1877 and 1879 transactions? Were industrial properties already legally separated? What is the exact primary title chain?

2. Borek factory and industrial land

Strousberg era
Documented industrial system

Borek within the Zbiroh industrial complex

Borek formed a major component of Strousberg's iron, steel, engineering and railway-production system.

30 Nov 1877
Reported execution auction

Industrial works auctioned separately

A Czech industrial-history account records an execution auction covering Borek, Holoubkov, Dobřív, Strašice and Františkov. This is important evidence that the industrial properties may have followed a legal chain distinct from the landed estate.

c. 1879
Primary property evidence under study

Goldschmied names appear in cadastral material

Surviving Kařez/Borek property documentation contains names including Ludwig Goldschmied and Emil Goldschmied, together with a third person whose identity and role require verification. See the 1879 source analysis.

1880s onward
Chain unresolved

Roubíček / Brandeis transition

Later histories describe Roubíček and Brandeis interests and eventually E. Brandeis control. The precise sequence of legal title, leases and operating control remains to be reconstructed from primary records.

Open questions: Who received title at the 1877 execution auction? What did the Goldschmied entries represent? When and by what instrument did Brandeis acquire title or control?

3. Hotel Borek

c. 1871
Later historical accounts

Hotel associated with Strousberg's industrial settlement

Local and heritage histories associate Hotel Borek čp. 39 with the Strousberg development period.

After 1875
Unresolved

Which property chain did the hotel follow?

It is not yet established whether the hotel followed the landed estate, the industrial properties, or a separate parcel/title chain after Strousberg's collapse.

Late 19th century
Secondary claim only

Reported Tichý ownership

A later local account reports an intermediate owner as “J. Tichý, owner of a Prague variety theatre,” before E. Brandeis. The identity and ownership claim have not been confirmed by a primary title record; other evidence identifies Eduard/Edvard Tichý with Prague's Théâtre Variété, creating an additional discrepancy.

Brandeis era
Ownership/control requires title record

Hotel becomes associated with E. Brandeis

Later accounts and factory material connect the hotel with the Borek enterprise. The land-book transfer into Brandeis ownership remains a key target.

Open questions: Was Tichý owner, operator or neither? Who held title immediately after Strousberg? Did the hotel move with Borek industrial property?

4. Holoubkov works and carriage production

1869–1875
Well-supported industrial history

Expansion under Strousberg

Holoubkov's official municipal history records expansion of the ironworks and construction of a railway-wagon factory under Strousberg.

30 Nov 1877
Reported execution auction

Holoubkov included with industrial works

The industrial-history account places Holoubkov in the same execution auction as Borek and other works.

Later 1870s–1880s
Conflicting/partial secondary accounts

Hopfengärtner involvement

Holoubkov municipal history says Max Hopfengärtner, formerly a leading Strousberg associate, subsequently bought the works. Other accounts describe intervening bank ownership and leasing arrangements. Primary land and corporate records are needed to distinguish purchase, lease and operating control.

Open questions: Did the Vienna mortgage institution take title in 1877? When did Hopfengärtner acquire legal ownership? What parcels belonged to the carriage works rather than the wider estate?

5. Bubny wagon works, Prague

1870
Corporate history

Prague–Vienna wagon company established

The Prag-Wiener Aktiengesellschaft für Fabrikation von Waggons und Eisenbahnbedarf operated a major wagon works in Bubny.

1874
Ownership status disputed

Strousberg takes over / contracts to acquire the works

Historical accounts agree that Bubny became part of Strousberg's production system for Russian railway-carriage contracts, but descriptions of the legal transaction differ. Some say he acquired the company; another detailed account describes a June 1874 sale agreement for the factory buildings and says completion failed.

1875
Operational connection documented

Collapse leaves the Bubny relationship unresolved

Strousberg's collapse interrupted the enterprise. The distinction between ownership of shares, ownership of buildings/land and operational control is central here.

1877–1879
Corporate disposal reported

Liquidation and later disposal

Corporate histories describe liquidation and disposal of the wagon company's real estate after the Strousberg collapse.

Open questions: Did Strousberg ever become registered proprietor of the Bubny land or buildings? Did he instead control the company? What exactly did the 9 June 1874 agreement convey?

6. Railway and Stanice Zbiroh property

19th century
Research required

Railway rights through the industrial landscape

Strousberg constructed private industrial railway infrastructure connecting parts of his production system. The rights over land, sidings and connections need to be distinguished from ownership of the later station property.

1912 era
Separate title questions

The later Zbiroh station

The station raises at least four independent questions: who owned the land, who commissioned the station, who financed construction, and which railway entity operated it. These should not be collapsed into a single ownership claim.

Open questions: What land was transferred for the station? Who paid for the station and associated infrastructure? What rights did the railway obtain over neighbouring industrial property?

Evidence still needed

The highest-priority records are the pre- and post-1875 land books for the relevant cadastral areas; the 1877 execution-auction protocols for the industrial works; the 1879 Zbiroh auction/adjudication and conveyance; mortgage and creditor schedules; the Bubny 1874 agreement and company register; Hotel Borek title entries; and the corporate/property files documenting the Roubíček, Brandeis, Goldschmied and Hopfengärtner transitions.

The page will be updated as those records are located. A missing document is recorded as a gap, not as evidence that a transfer did or did not occur.

Working sources

This first version draws on contemporary financial reporting, August Sedláček's historical account of Zbiroh, Czech industrial histories, Holoubkov municipal history, Bubny railway-industrial research, the archive's own cadastral material and the existing Strousberg, Brandeis and Goldschmied source pages. Each ownership chain will be strengthened as original deeds, land-book entries and auction records are obtained.
