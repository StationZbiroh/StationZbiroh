# Research Methodology

## Purpose

The Stanice Zbiroh Historical Research Archive documents the station, Borek industrial landscape, neighbouring communities, people and institutions through evidence-led historical research.

The methodological benchmark is rigorous documentary history: evidence first, context second, interpretation third, conclusion last. The archive does not collect documents merely to confirm an existing story.

## Evidence status

Every substantial historical claim should, where practical, be assigned one of these states:

- **Documented** — directly supported by identifiable primary evidence.
- **Strongly supported** — supported by multiple reliable sources, though primary documentation may be incomplete.
- **Plausible hypothesis** — consistent with available evidence but not demonstrated.
- **Disputed** — credible sources or evidence conflict.
- **Unverified** — reported or suspected but presently lacking adequate evidence.

## Source hierarchy

1. Primary archival records, contemporary maps, cadastral records, photographs, company registers, railway records and contemporary publications.
2. Scholarly and institutional secondary research.
3. Local histories and later recollections, identified as such.
4. Working research notes and hypotheses.

A lower category is not automatically false. Its evidentiary status must simply remain visible.

## Provenance and links

Records should preserve, where available: archive/institution; collection or fonds; reference/signature; document title; creator; date; language; page/image number; stable external URL; access date; and a local archival record ID.

External links should point to the originating archive, catalogue, authority record, museum, database or scholarly resource whenever possible. Links are evidence trails, not substitutes for citations.

## Contradictions and corrections

Contradictory evidence is retained and described. Uncertainty is not silently resolved. If new evidence changes a conclusion, the interpretation should be revised while the repository history preserves the earlier state.

## Working rule

**Evidence → context → interpretation → conclusion.**

The strength of the language used on the public site must not exceed the strength of the evidence.

## Editorial position

The archive is maintained at the former Zbiroh station by its owner and restorer. This provides direct access to the building, the surrounding landscape and privately held material. It may also create interests that should be visible to readers. Historical claims are therefore assessed by the quality and provenance of their evidence, not by whether they support a present-day position.

Material concerning current property, planning or administrative questions must be identified as such and kept analytically separate from historical reconstruction. The archive does not treat present ownership as proof of a historical interpretation.

## Evidence-status vocabulary

Public pages should use the following terms consistently:

- **Documented** — directly supported by an identified primary record or physical object.
- **Strong secondary evidence** — supported by a reputable later source, while the underlying primary record has not yet been examined by this project.
- **Working interpretation** — a reasoned reading of evidence that remains open to revision.
- **Hypothesis** — a proposition being tested, not an established conclusion.
- **Research lead** — potentially relevant information that has not yet been sufficiently verified.
- **Unresolved** — evidence is absent, incomplete or contradictory.

## Minimum source record

Where the information is available, each catalogued item should record:

1. Creator, author or issuing body.
2. Original title or supplied descriptive title.
3. Date, including whether the date is printed, inferred or approximate.
4. Type of item: letter, postcard, photograph, map, register, catalogue, article or other form.
5. Holding institution, collection or private custodian.
6. Fonds, collection, box, file, signature, folio or page reference.
7. How and when the research copy was obtained.
8. Whether digital images or transcriptions are complete, cropped, enhanced or otherwise altered.
9. Rights or reuse information when known.
10. Related archive pages and the claims for which the item is being used.

Missing fields should be marked as unknown or still sought. They should not be silently inferred.

## Transcription, translation and image handling

- Preserve original spelling, punctuation and abbreviations in diplomatic transcriptions unless editorial changes are explicitly marked.
- Put supplied expansions, uncertain readings and omissions in square brackets.
- Identify the translator and distinguish translation from transcription.
- Keep a link or reference to the source image beside any transcription or translation whenever practical.
- Record substantial cropping, contrast adjustment, colour correction, stitching or machine enhancement.
- Treat OCR and machine translation as research aids until checked against the source.

## Conflicting evidence and corrections

Contradictory evidence is retained rather than harmonised for narrative convenience. A revised conclusion should explain what new evidence caused the change. Git version history records alterations to public pages, but it is not a substitute for authenticating the historical sources themselves.

Corrections should be incorporated without erasing the existence of a genuine earlier uncertainty. Material corrections should identify the affected claim and the evidence supporting the change.

## Citation practice

Claims should be cited as close as practical to the relevant sentence or paragraph. A bibliography is useful but does not replace a precise page, folio, catalogue or URL reference for a particular claim. When this archive links to a digitised copy hosted elsewhere, readers should cite the original work or holding institution as well as the archive's analysis.

Suggested citation for an archive page:

> “Page title,” *Stanice Zbiroh Historical Research Archive*, page URL (accessed day month year).
