Hotel Borek · Source-object analysis

More Details About the Postcard

A close reading of the printed wording, photographer’s imprint, handwriting, address field, postage stamp and Zbiroh cancellation on the reverse of the Hotel Borek postcard.

Printed text

HOTEL BOREK
Zbiroh - Nádraží.
Velký sál měří 405 m².

English translation

HOTEL BOREK
Zbiroh — Station.
The Great Hall measures 405 m².

Nádraží literally means railway station. The postcard therefore markets the hotel through its association with the Zbiroh railway station and singles out the 405 m² Great Hall as a principal feature.

Photographer and printed dating evidence

Studio imprint
Fotografický atelier M. Bickert, Rokycany — “Photographic studio M. Bickert, Rokycany.”
Printed number/date
The vertical imprint ends with 1103–1911. The final element strongly supports a 1911 production or publication date for this postcard, while 1103 is most cautiously treated as a studio, printing or catalogue number unless further records identify its exact purpose.

The postage stamp

The card bears a green 5 heller Austrian stamp showing Emperor Franz Joseph I. It belongs to the Austrian 1908 Jubilee issue, created for the sixtieth anniversary of Franz Joseph’s reign. The 5 heller stamp was issued in 1908; the design is associated with Koloman Moser, with engraving by Ferdinand Schirnböck.

The year of a stamp issue is not automatically the year a postcard was mailed: stamps could remain in use after issue. Here, however, the 1908 stamp is fully compatible with the postcard’s separate printed 1911 dating evidence.

Reference: Graphikportal / MAK Vienna — 1908 Austrian 5 Heller Jubilee stamp.

Postmark and place of mailing

The circular cancellation clearly reads ZBIROH, establishing that the card passed through the Zbiroh postal system. The numerical date inside the cancellation is heavily obscured by the dark cancellation over the green stamp in the surviving photograph, so an exact postal date should not yet be claimed from the postmark alone.

A faint handwritten notation along the upper edge appears possibly to include a date resembling 23/4 911 — potentially 23 April 1911 — but the writing is too faint to publish that as a confirmed date. It is retained as a transcription hypothesis for further examination of the original card.

Recipient and address

The right half contains the recipient’s name and address in cursive handwriting. Individual strokes and several words are visible, but the current photograph does not support a reliable full reading of the addressee or destination. Rather than silently guessing the name or place, the archive records the address as present but not yet securely transcribed.

If a higher-resolution scan or raking-light photograph becomes available, the recipient and destination may be recoverable and could provide a valuable clue to the social network around Hotel Borek.

Handwritten message

A substantial handwritten message occupies the left half of the card. Its ink or pencil has faded considerably and overlaps the printed heading. Some isolated forms can be seen, but not enough of the message can presently be read with confidence to justify a continuous translation.

Archive rule: uncertain handwriting is not reconstructed as if it were known. Future readings should show uncertain letters in brackets or with question marks and should preserve the original language before giving an English translation.

What can currently be dated with confidence?

Stamp issue
1908 — Austrian 5 heller Franz Joseph Jubilee issue.
Postcard imprint
1911 appears in the photographer/studio imprint as part of “1103–1911,” strongly supporting a 1911 postcard production/publication context.
Mailing place
Zbiroh, confirmed by the circular cancellation.
Exact mailing day
Not yet securely established. A faint handwritten notation may read approximately “23/4 911,” but this remains provisional because the postal cancellation date itself is obscured.

Why this reverse matters

The back of the postcard contributes several independent layers of evidence at once: it identifies Hotel Borek; links it explicitly with Zbiroh-Nádraží; advertises the 405 m² Great Hall; identifies a photographic studio in Rokycany; provides evidence consistent with 1911; preserves a Zbiroh postal cancellation; and contains a still-unresolved personal message and address. It is therefore not merely the reverse of an illustration but a compact historical document in its own right.
