People and organisations

A name and entity directory for the railway, industrial, financial and property history surrounding Zbiroh station and Borek. Variant spellings are included to improve discovery while uncertain identities and relationships remain explicitly marked.

← Historical Research Archive

People make the landscape: this directory is being expanded beyond prominent owners to document the workers, residents, tenants, engineers, railway staff, tradespeople and families who lived or worked around the station. Each profile should distinguish a documented local residence or workplace from a family, corporate or research connection. Archival records and contributions from descendants will be added as they are found.

How to use this directory. A name appearing in the same document as another person does not automatically establish kinship, ownership or representation. Follow the linked research pages for evidence status and sources.

Bethel Henry Strousberg

Also Baruch Hirsch Strausberg; Henry Bethel von Strousberg; B. H. Strousberg; “the Railway King.”

Railway entrepreneur and developer of the Zbirow industrial system from 1868 until its collapse in 1875. Research profile · 1875 industrial report

Franz Rziha

Also rendered Ržiha or Ržiha in some catalogues.

Chief engineer who signed the 1 March 1875 technical and financial assessment of the Zbirow industrial works for H. v. Goldschmidt & Co. Report and transcription

H. v. Goldschmidt & Co.

Vienna banking house; Goldschmidt & Co.; spelling distinct from Goldschmied.

Prospective financier and recipient of the 1875 Zbirow assessment. No acquisition of the Borek factory or relationship with Emil and Ludwig Goldschmied has yet been proved. Memoir context

Emil and Ludwig Goldschmied

Sons of Abraham and Theresia Goldschmied according to the family-history material under review.

Named with Jakob Roubíček in 1879 property material near Zbiroh station. The archive is testing inheritance, succession and representation hypotheses. Family research · 1879 evidence

Jakob Roubíček

Also Jakob Roubicek; later sources use Roubíček without a given name.

Appears with the Goldschmied brothers in 1879 evidence and with Brandeis in the reported 1881 factory acquisition. His possible representative, guardian, creditor or ownership role remains unresolved. Evidence and questions

Emil Brandeis

Also E. Brandeis; identity may refer to more than one generation in different records.

Associated with the Borek ironworks and later Brandeis–Eisenschimmel concern. The archive separates the documented business name from unresolved biographical identification. Research profile · Factory catalogue

Franz Eisenschimmel

Engineer; Franz Eisenschimmel & Co.; Brandeis–Eisenschimmel.

Founder and namesake of the Roudnice agricultural-machinery factory whose production was transferred to Borek following a 1918 agreement with Brandeis. Biographical profile · Company evidence

Dr Valtr Eisenschimmel

Also Walter Eisenschimmel; born 12 February 1902.

Chemist and specialist assistant at the German Technical University in Prague; son of Ludwig Eisenschimmel and first cousin of Franz Eisenschimmel. Deported with his wife Helena and son Tomáš to Terezín in July 1943 and to Auschwitz in October 1944; none returned. Biographical profile

Mančík and Šlesinger

Spellings without Czech diacritics may occur in catalogues and transcriptions.

Reported by a later industrial history as purchasers of the Borek factory following Strousberg’s collapse; the original transaction is still sought. Property reconstruction

Max Hopfengärtner

Former Strousberg associate linked by later accounts to the post-collapse Holoubkov works. The exact ownership and leasing sequence requires primary records. Property reconstruction

Josef Colloredo-Mannsfeld

Reported purchaser of the Zbiroh landed estate in 1879. The archive distinguishes that estate transaction from the separate industrial-property chain. Property reconstruction

J. Tichý

A local account names “J. Tichý”; other evidence points toward Eduard or Edvard Tichý, so the identity remains disputed.

Reported intermediate owner of Hotel Borek before Brandeis. Hotel chronology

Jan Heindl and Vendelín Hron

Railway architects connected by published evidence with the attribution problem surrounding the 1911–1913 station project. The original station plans remain decisive evidence. Station chronology

Richard Aurich

Inventor or technical figure connected with silo and green-fodder preservation technology documented in Borek materials. Aurich research
