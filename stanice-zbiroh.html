A chronological, evidence-led history of the former Zbiroh railway station: its construction, ownership, passenger life, changing landscape, closure and survival.

How this page works

The history is presented as one chronological timeline. Ownership, railway operation, changes to the surrounding landscape and the later fate of the building are placed together so that each period can be understood in context. Where the evidence does not yet establish an exact date or institutional transition, the gap is explicitly marked as an open research question.

Chronological timeline

1862

The railway predates the present building

The railway through this area belongs to an earlier phase of railway history than the monumental station building shown on this page. The distinction matters: the present building is not the beginning of railway history at Zbiroh.

Context · further primary sourcing to be added
October 1911

A State Railways Directorate project

Heritage documentation identifies the project for the new station with the Ředitelství státních drah v Praze (State Railways Directorate in Prague), dated October 1911. This establishes the building as a state-railway project rather than a municipal building belonging to the town of Zbiroh. The builder is identified as Prague builder Vendelín Hron.

1911–1912

The present station complex is built

The new Zbiroh station was constructed in the late Secession style during the doubling and reconstruction of the Prague–Plzeň railway. Although named for Zbiroh, the building stands in the cadastral territory of Kařez.

1913

Passenger life in the new station

A railway account published when passenger operation ended states that Zbiroh station had handled passengers from 1913. This provides a useful working date for passenger use associated with the present monumental station building.

Early 20th century · exact postcard date under research

Station, Hotel Borek and the earlier landscape

The postcard above preserves the physical relationship between Hotel Borek, the station and the surrounding countryside. The foreground appears low-lying and wet, while the slopes are substantially more open and sparsely wooded than today. The image establishes the visual difference; it does not establish why the tree cover was different.

1918 onward

From the Habsburg state railway system into Czechoslovakia

The political and railway administration surrounding the station changed with the creation of Czechoslovakia. The archive has not yet assembled the complete primary-document ownership and administrative chain for the building through the successive state-railway institutions.

Open research question · exact institutional ownership chain
20th century

A working railway station

For most of the twentieth century the building continued in railway use. Photographs, plans, staffing records, alterations and other evidence will be inserted into this part of the timeline as they are identified and dated.

Research in progress
October 1992

Construction of the D5 motorway begins nearby

Published D5 construction history records the start of work on the Bavoryně–Mýto section in October 1992. The motorway works would substantially transform the older landscape around the station.

26 October 1995

The D5 opens through the area

The Bavoryně–Kařez and Kařez–Mýto sections of the D5 were both opened to traffic on 26 October 1995. Comparison with the early postcard provides a particularly clear illustration of the transformation of the station landscape. The precise relationship between the wet ground visible in the postcard and the later motorway footprint remains under investigation.

By 2012

České dráhy owns the station building

Contemporary reporting identifies České dráhy (Czech Railways) as the owner of the station building when its railway function was coming to an end. The archive still needs primary records documenting each institutional transfer between the original state-railway period and České dráhy.

Night of 27–28 April 2012

Passenger operation at Zbiroh ends

Passenger operation at Zbiroh ended during the night of 27–28 April 2012. The new Kařez stop entered service on 28 April and passenger trains transferred there. An SŽDC project press release identifies the first train stopping at Kařez as R 828 at 00:34. Contemporary railway reporting described the change as the end of an era for a station that had handled passengers since 1913 — almost a century.

2012

The redundant building is prepared for sale

Contemporary reports from April and July 2012 state that České dráhy intended to dispose of the now-redundant station property. The historic building had become separated from the main passenger function during the railway-corridor reconstruction.

After 2012

From railway property to a new life

The next stage of the timeline concerns the sale of the station, subsequent private ownership, preservation of the protected building and the changing relationship between the former station complex and the operational railway. Exact transfer dates and documentary evidence will be added as they are established.

Research in progress
Present

A historical building and research archive

The former station survives as a physical record of more than a century of change in railway, industrial and landscape history. This timeline will continue to expand as primary documents, photographs and ownership records are located.

Source-image archive

Historical station photographs and postcards are being assembled in a dedicated source-image collection. Images will be presented with dates and provenance where known, and uncertain dating will be identified as such.

Sources and evidence

  1. Památkový katalog / heritage record, Zbiroh railway station. Basis for the 1911–1912 construction date, late-Secession classification, State Railways Directorate in Prague project of October 1911, and builder Vendelín Hron. See also the Atlas české architektury record reproducing the heritage history: železniční stanice Zbiroh, Kařez.
  2. Kařez historical StoryMap. Summarises the station chronicle and heritage record, including the station's cadastral location in Kařez, the origin of the Zbiroh name, the 1911–1912 building history, and its relationship to Hotel Borek and Železárna Borek: Výlet Kařez – Třenická rozhledna.
  3. České dálnice, D5 construction history. Records commencement of the Bavoryně–Mýto construction in October 1992 and opening of both Bavoryně–Kařez and Kařez–Mýto on 26 October 1995: Dálnice D5.
  4. SŽDC press release, 28 April 2012, “Optimalizace trati Zbiroh – Rokycany: Nová zastávka Kařez v provozu”. Contemporary project source for the opening of Kařez on 28 April 2012 and the first stopping train, R 828 at 00:34: press release copy.
  5. Český rozhlas Plzeň, 27 April 2012. Contemporary report that Zbiroh was closing to passengers and being replaced by Kařez; it also reports that České dráhy would offer the redundant station building for sale: Nádraží ve Zbirohu nahradí zastávka Kařez.
  6. ŽelPage, 2 May 2012. Contemporary railway account recording the midnight 27/28 April transfer of passenger stops to Kařez and stating that Zbiroh had handled passengers since 1913: Nová zastávka Kařez již slouží cestujícím.
  7. ČT24, 5 April 2012. Contemporary report identifying České dráhy as preparing to sell the Zbiroh station building after trains ceased stopping there: ČD prodávají budovy, zbaví se i celého nádraží.
  8. iDNES, 24 July 2012. Identifies České dráhy as the then owner and seller of the former Zbiroh station building and associated land: Dráhy rozprodávají majetek.
  9. Stanice Zbiroh Historical Research Archive postcard. Original historical postcard captioned Zbiroh. Nové nádraží. Hotel Borek., reproduced on this page. It is the primary visual source for the early relationship of the station, hotel and surrounding landscape. Exact postcard date remains under research.

Research principle: where these sources disagree, are imprecise, or do not establish a point, the page treats the matter as unresolved rather than silently reconciling it.
