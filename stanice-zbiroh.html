How this page works

This page separates documented evidence from interpretation. Historical photographs, postcards, plans, maps and archival records are arranged chronologically. Where a date, event, ownership detail or explanation has not yet been established from surviving evidence, it remains an open research question.

The original postcard

Who owned the station?

The surviving heritage record identifies the 1911–1912 building as a project of the Ředitelství státních drah v Praze (State Railways Directorate in Prague), constructed by Prague builder Vendelín Hron. That establishes the station as a state-railway project, rather than a building belonging to the town of Zbiroh; geographically it stands in the cadastral territory of Kařez.

For the later period, contemporary reports establish that the station building was owned by České dráhy (Czech Railways). When passenger operation ended in 2012, České dráhy announced that the redundant building would be sold, and it was subsequently offered as ČD property. The archive is still assembling the documentary ownership chain between the original state-railway period and České dráhy, so that intermediate institutional changes are not compressed into an unsupported single ownership statement.

Chronology

1911–1912

The new Zbiroh station

The present station complex was built in the late Secession style during the doubling and reconstruction of the Prague–Plzeň railway. Heritage documentation dates it to 1911–1912 and identifies the October 1911 project with the State Railways Directorate in Prague and the builder as Vendelín Hron.

1913

Passenger use of the new station building

A contemporary railway report published at the end of passenger operation states that Zbiroh station had handled passengers from 1913. This provides a useful distinction between the older railway history of the location and passenger use associated with the present monumental station building.

Early 20th century · exact postcard date under research

Station and Hotel Borek before the motorway

The postcard above preserves the physical relationship between the new station, Hotel Borek and the surrounding landscape. The foreground appears low-lying and wet, while the slopes behind the buildings are substantially more open and sparsely wooded than today. The photograph establishes the difference in landscape; the reason for the difference in tree cover has not yet been established.

October 1992–26 October 1995

The D5 transforms the landscape

Published construction history for the D5 records that work on the Bavoryně–Mýto section began in October 1992. Its Bavoryně–Kařez and Kařez–Mýto sections were both opened to traffic on 26 October 1995. The motorway substantially changed the landscape around the station. The precise relationship between the wet ground visible in the historical postcard and the later motorway footprint remains a subject for map and cadastral comparison.

27–28 April 2012

Passenger operation ends

Passenger operation at Zbiroh ended during the night of 27–28 April 2012. The new Kařez stop entered service on 28 April, and passenger trains that had served Zbiroh transferred there. A contemporary SŽDC project press release identifies the first train stopping at Kařez as R 828 at 00:34. A railway report described the change as the end of the era of passenger traffic at Zbiroh, which it said had served passengers since 1913.

2012 onward

České dráhy prepare the building for sale

Contemporary reporting from April and July 2012 identifies České dráhy as the owner disposing of the now-redundant station property. The historic building had become separated from the main passenger function as part of the railway-corridor works.

Research in progress

Ownership, later use and survival

The archive is continuing to document the complete institutional and ownership chain, the subsequent private ownership of the building, alterations to the site, and the relationship between the protected station complex and the modern railway.

Source-image archive

Historical station photographs and postcards are being assembled in a dedicated source-image collection. Images will be presented with dates and provenance where known, and uncertain dating will be identified as such.

Sources and evidence

  1. Památkový katalog / heritage record, Zbiroh railway station. Basis for the 1911–1912 construction date, late-Secession classification, State Railways Directorate in Prague project of October 1911, and builder Vendelín Hron. See also the Atlas české architektury record reproducing the heritage history: železniční stanice Zbiroh, Kařez.
  2. Kařez historical StoryMap. Summarises the station chronicle and heritage record, including the station's cadastral location in Kařez, the origin of the Zbiroh name, the 1911–1912 building history, and its relationship to Hotel Borek and Železárna Borek: Výlet Kařez – Třenická rozhledna.
  3. České dálnice, D5 construction history. Records commencement of the Bavoryně–Mýto construction in October 1992 and opening of both Bavoryně–Kařez and Kařez–Mýto on 26 October 1995: Dálnice D5.
  4. SŽDC press release, 28 April 2012, “Optimalizace trati Zbiroh – Rokycany: Nová zastávka Kařez v provozu”. Contemporary project source for the opening of Kařez on 28 April 2012 and the first stopping train, R 828 at 00:34: press release copy.
  5. Český rozhlas Plzeň, 27 April 2012. Contemporary report that Zbiroh was closing to passengers and being replaced by Kařez; it also reports that České dráhy would offer the redundant station building for sale: Nádraží ve Zbirohu nahradí zastávka Kařez.
  6. ŽelPage, 2 May 2012. Contemporary railway account recording the midnight 27/28 April transfer of passenger stops to Kařez and stating that Zbiroh had handled passengers since 1913: Nová zastávka Kařez již slouží cestujícím.
  7. ČT24, 5 April 2012. Contemporary report identifying České dráhy as preparing to sell the Zbiroh station building after trains ceased stopping there: ČD prodávají budovy, zbaví se i celého nádraží.
  8. iDNES, 24 July 2012. Identifies České dráhy as the then owner and seller of the former Zbiroh station building and associated land: Dráhy rozprodávají majetek.
  9. Stanice Zbiroh Historical Research Archive postcard. Original historical postcard captioned Zbiroh. Nové nádraží. Hotel Borek., reproduced on this page. It is the primary visual source for the early relationship of the station, hotel and surrounding landscape. Exact postcard date remains under research.

Research principle: where these sources disagree, are imprecise, or do not establish a point, the page treats the matter as unresolved rather than silently reconciling it.
