Franz Eisenschimmel

An engineer, industrial founder and namesake of Franz Eisenschimmel & Co., the Roudnice agricultural-machinery enterprise whose production became part of the combined Brandeis–Eisenschimmel works at Borek.

Profession

Engineer and agricultural-machinery manufacturer

Family

Son of Emil and Rosa Eisenschimmel; nephew of Ludwig; first cousin of Dr Valtr Eisenschimmel

Company

Franz Eisenschimmel & Co., Roudnice nad Labem

Emigration

Left Europe with his wife Rosalie in 1914; became a United States citizen in 1926

An engineer builds a machinery works

The Mladá Boleslav family history identifies Franz as the eldest child of Emil Eisenschimmel and Rosa Eislerová and records that he became an engineer. A specialist history of agricultural machinery dates the foundation of Franz Eisenschimmel & Co. in Roudnice nad Labem to approximately 1905.

The works was substantial: three large halls and a machine hall with a boiler house. Its documented product range included chaff cutters, horse-driven gears, beet cutters, and wine and fruit presses. Creating an enterprise of this scale—and placing his own name upon it—is Franz’s clearest documented industrial achievement.

The people behind the company

A Roudnice industrial record names engineer Franz Eisenschimmel, Prague merchant Ludwig Eisenschimmel, Heinrich E. Eisenschimmel and Rudolf Bächer as partners in the firm in 1905. This shows that Franz’s enterprise drew upon a wider partnership and family network rather than the work of one man alone.

The family history records that Franz and Rosalie left for overseas in 1914 and became United States citizens in 1926. This is an important transatlantic connection, but the evidence currently available does not establish that American expertise created the Roudnice machinery designs; the factory already existed before their departure.

From Roudnice to Borek

c. 1905
Franz Eisenschimmel & Co. is established at Roudnice nad Labem.
1914
Franz and Rosalie leave Europe for overseas.
1918
Industrial-topography research records an agreement between Brandeis and the company Franz Eisenschimmel & Co. to transfer agricultural-machinery production from Roudnice to Borek.
1919–c. 1920
Corporate and technical publications document the combined Borek and Eisenschimmel enterprise.
1926
Franz and Rosalie become United States citizens.

Important distinction: the evidence identifies the 1918 agreement as an agreement with the company bearing Franz’s name. Because Franz had left Europe in 1914, the archive does not yet claim that he personally negotiated the Borek transaction. Company records or correspondence are needed to establish his individual role.

Why he belongs in this archive

Franz supplied the engineering and company identity represented by the second half of the name Brandeis–Eisenschimmel. His Roudnice works brought an established agricultural-machinery range into the industrial landscape at Borek, where the enlarged enterprise became closely connected with Zbiroh station.

His biography also connects the factory to a Jewish industrial family dispersed across Europe and the United States. His first cousin Dr Valtr Eisenschimmel, a chemist, was later deported with his family to Terezín and Auschwitz.
