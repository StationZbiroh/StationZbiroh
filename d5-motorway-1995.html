D5 Motorway 1995

Planning, construction and landscape change around Kařez and the former Zbiroh station.

Research position. The motorway is treated here as a landscape object in its own right. The page separates the documented construction chronology from interpretation of how the road changed the older station setting. The exact date of the postcard remains under research; its attribution as probably pre-war is based in part on the substantially less-developed landscape visible around the station.

Why the D5 belongs in the station history

The D5 did not merely add a road near Zbiroh station. Its construction introduced a major east–west transport corridor into a landscape that had developed around the railway, Hotel Borek, the Borek industrial area, local roads, fields and low-lying ground. For the archive, the motorway therefore marks a major late-20th-century change in the physical setting of the station.

The historical postcard above is useful because it records that setting before the motorway and before later development. Read together with maps, aerial photography and road-construction records, it can help reconstruct what was changed, crossed, removed or visually separated when the D5 was implemented.

Documented construction chronology

Late 1930s · broader historical context

Earlier motorway ideas

The Kařez municipal chronicle summary records that ideas for a motorway toward Plzeň and Germany were already being discussed at the end of the 1930s. This is useful local context, but it should not be read as evidence that the final D5 alignment at Kařez had already been fixed at that time.

1977

Construction of the D5 begins elsewhere

The wider D5 construction programme began in 1977 with work associated with the Beroun bridge. The route was opened progressively westward over the following decades.

October 1992

Bavoryně–Mýto construction begins

Construction of motorway project 0505, Bavoryně–Mýto, began in October 1992. The documented project length was 21.538 km and it was divided into two principal parts: Bavoryně–Kařez, 13.1 km, and Kařez–Mýto, 8.4 km.

21 June 1993

Traffic pressure recorded at Kařez

A published summary drawn from the Kařez municipal chronicle records a municipal meeting describing the state road through Kařez as heavily congested with columns of cars and lorries in both directions. This provides local evidence for the conditions against which the new motorway was being constructed.

14 July 1994–29 June 1995

Phased opening near Mýto

For the western end of project 0505, the existing I/5 road was incorporated into one half of the motorway between km 48.000 and 50.238. This required staged construction and traffic transfers. One half-profile opened on 14 July 1994 and the full profile on this part followed on 29 June 1995.

26 October 1995

Bavoryně–Kařez and Kařez–Mýto open

Both principal parts of the Bavoryně–Mýto project were opened to traffic on 26 October 1995. Contemporary D5 construction data record the road as category D 26.5/120. The project included the Žebrák and Cerhovice grade-separated junctions and the large Záluží service area.

1995 onward

A new transport landscape

The opening shifted long-distance road traffic away from the older route through Kařez and established the motorway as a permanent element beside the historical railway landscape. The archive's next task is to document the transformation parcel by parcel and view by view rather than relying only on retrospective description.

The landscape question

The central local question is not simply when the D5 opened, but what its implementation did to the earlier station landscape. The postcard shows a broad, open setting around the station before the motorway. Later road construction, associated earthworks and subsequent development changed how the station relates visually and physically to the surrounding land.

Future additions to this page should therefore prioritise the motorway plans and route reservations, cadastral and land-acquisition evidence, construction photographs, dated aerial surveys, changes to local access and drainage, and photographs from matching viewpoints before and after construction. These records will allow the archive to distinguish visible change from assumptions about why individual changes occurred.
